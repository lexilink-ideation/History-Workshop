The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🛡️

When Spartan boys reached the age of twenty, they had to pass a special test of fitness and bravery. If they passed, they were finally allowed to join the Spartan army — and they would remain soldiers for the rest of their lives, all the way into old age. Even if a soldier got married, he was not allowed to live with his family. Instead, he lived with the other men in military barracks.

By the time a young Spartan man reached this test, he had already spent thirteen years training in the camps, ever since he was taken from his family at the age of seven. Every year of marching, fighting, and enduring hunger had been preparation for this single, decisive moment.

Boys who failed the test faced a harsh future. They were never allowed to vote, and they could never become full citizens of Sparta. In Spartan society, being a warrior was almost everything.

But what about the girls of Sparta? They too were taught to exercise and grow strong — not so they could become soldiers themselves, but so that they could one day become the mothers of strong sons who would fight for Sparta. Spartans believed that women were more timid and weaker than men, so women held a lower place in Spartan society than men did.

A Spartan mother sending her son off to battle once told him, "Come back with your shield, or on it!" Since defeated soldiers had to give up their shields, she really meant: "Either win the battle, or come home dead."

Spartan mothers were expected to praise their sons for warlike courage, and to reward them for bravery rather than comfort them for pain. Spartans would rather die in battle than live with the shame of losing.

Sparta was never known for beautiful art or great storytelling — but the Spartan army became feared across the entire ancient world for its bravery and toughness. Even today, English speakers still use the word "spartan" to describe someone who bears pain or hardship without ever complaining.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 "even if" for unexpected conditions
We use "even if" to show that something remains true no matter what happens.
Even if a soldier got married, he still lived in the barracks.
Even if boys were exhausted, they were never allowed to complain.
A Spartan would keep fighting even if he was badly hurt.
Tip: "Even if" introduces a surprising condition that does not change the main result — unlike "if," which shows a normal condition.
📌 "rather...than" for preference
We use "rather...than" to show that one thing is strongly preferred over another.
Spartans would rather die than lose a battle.
A mother would rather praise bravery than comfort pain.
Spartans preferred toughness rather than comfort.
Tip: Pattern: would rather + base verb + than + base verb. It expresses a strong preference between two choices.
📌 Reported Imperative — "told him to..."
We can report a command using "told + person + to + verb."
The mother told her son to come home with his shield.
Spartan law told citizens to put the army first.
Officers told young men to pass the fitness test.
Tip: Pattern: told + person + to + base verb. This turns a direct command into a reported sentence.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🛡️
A society’s greatest fear can shape its whole way of life. Sparta organized its entire society around the fear of losing in battle. What does a society built entirely around one fear gain, and what might it lose?
👩
Not all strength looks the same. Spartan women were seen as weaker because they did not fight, even though they endured childbirth, ran households, and raised soldiers. Do you think physical fighting is the only kind of strength that matters?
🗣️
Language keeps history alive. We still use the word "spartan" today, thousands of years later. What other words do you know that come from ancient history?
⚖️
Every value has a cost. Sparta gained a feared, disciplined army, but its people had almost no art, music, or personal freedom. Do you think this was a fair trade?

💡 Big Idea

Sparta built an entire society, for both men and women, around the goal of raising the toughest, bravest soldiers possible — a value so powerful it still gives us a word in English today. A society’s deepest values can shape the lives of every person in it, from birth to death.
