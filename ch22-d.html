The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🗳️

Spartans were expected to obey their king without question. But the Greeks who lived in Athens did things very differently. In Athens, every citizen had a real say in how the city was run, because Athens was a true democracy.

This meant that whenever a new law was proposed, the citizens of Athens could vote on whether or not it should actually be followed. Each citizen had the chance to say, "Yes, this is a good law!" or "No, I disagree!" If more people voted yes than no, the law would pass. Athenians also voted for their leaders, decided how much tax they should pay, and even voted on whether to go to war.

Whenever it was time to vote, Athenian citizens gathered together in the middle of the city, at a special meeting place called a forum. There, they would argue passionately about the issue before finally making up their minds and voting.

Imagine a real example: perhaps a leader proposed building a new set of city walls, paid for with higher taxes. Some citizens would stand up at the forum and argue that the walls were necessary for safety. Others would argue that the tax was too high a price to pay. After everyone had spoken, the citizens would finally vote, and whichever side had the most votes would decide the outcome for the whole city.

For this system to work well, the citizens had to be educated. They needed to understand why taxes mattered, whether their leaders were making good decisions, and how the laws of their city actually worked. If citizens were completely ignorant, they would not be able to argue properly about their own government — and they would struggle to form a real opinion before voting.

This need for education would shape everything about how children were raised in Athens — and it would look nothing at all like the strict, silent military training of Spartan boys.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 First Conditional — Voting Rules
We use the first conditional to describe a rule or a real possible result.
If more people vote yes, the law will pass.
If citizens are ignorant, they will struggle to vote wisely.
If a leader governs badly, citizens will vote them out.
Tip: Pattern: If + present simple, ... will + base verb. This conditional describes a rule that is generally true or likely.
📌 Modal "could" for past ability/possibility
We use "could" to describe a general ability or possibility in the past.
Citizens could vote on new laws.
They could also vote for their leaders.
Anyone could share an opinion at the forum.
Tip: "Could" here describes something that was generally possible or allowed, not a single specific action.
📌 "in order to" for purpose
We use "in order to" (a more formal version of "to") to explain the purpose of an action.
Citizens had to be educated in order to vote wisely.
They studied laws in order to understand their government.
People gathered at the forum in order to debate new ideas.
Tip: "In order to" is followed by the base form of a verb and explains why something is done.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🗳️
Democracy depends on educated citizens. Athenians believed that voting only works well if citizens understand what they are voting about. Do you think this idea is still true in democracies today?
🗣️
Public debate can lead to better decisions. Athenians argued openly at the forum before voting. Why might open discussion lead to wiser choices than one person deciding alone?
👥
Ancient Athenian democracy did not include everyone. Only male citizens could vote in Athens — women, slaves, and foreigners could not. Why is it important to think critically about the limits of even a system we admire?
🌱
New ideas about government can start small. Athenian democracy began in one city, thousands of years ago, and its ideas still shape governments today. How can one small, new idea eventually change the whole world?

💡 Big Idea

In Athens, ordinary citizens — not a king — decided the laws, through public debate and voting, but this system only worked because Athenians believed strongly in education. A democracy is only as wise as the citizens who take part in it.
