The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
⚡

Look up at the night sky, and imagine the ancient Greeks looking up at the very same stars, thousands of years ago. The Greeks believed something remarkable: that a whole family of powerful beings lived on top of the tallest mountain in their land, watching everything they did.

The ancient Greeks were polytheists — they believed in many gods, not just one. This is very different from monotheists, like the ancient Jews, who worshipped only a single god. The Greeks believed their gods lived together on Mount Olympus, the highest mountain in all of Greece, and were extremely interested in what human beings were doing down on earth. Sometimes the gods were kind and generous. But at other times, they could be shockingly cruel.

Zeus, the chief god of all the Greek gods, is a perfect example. One day, Zeus looked down over Greece from Mount Olympus and saw men swarming like ants — cutting down trees, building houses, pulling fish from the sea. "There are too many people on the earth," he said gloomily. "I should get rid of some of them."

He thought and thought, and finally, he came up with a plan. He knew all the gods were going to a huge wedding soon — the perfect moment to start a fight. So Zeus made a golden apple so beautiful that it made the sun look dim, and wrote on it: "For the Most Beautiful." Then he called Eris, the goddess of strife, who loved to cause trouble. "Take this apple to the wedding," he told her, "and drop it in front of my wife, Hera."

At the wedding, Eris waited until Hera and two other powerful goddesses were standing together, chatting. Then she rolled the golden apple across the floor. It bumped against Hera's foot, and Hera picked it up. "For the Most Beautiful!" she read aloud. "Why, thank you! That's obviously me!" But Aphrodite, the goddess of love, twirled her golden hair and disagreed sweetly, while Athena, the goddess of war, reached for the apple too. "No!" Hera snapped, clutching it tightly. "It's mine!"

The argument grew louder, with guests taking sides about which goddess truly deserved the apple. Finally, Hera suggested Zeus should judge them. But Zeus, standing innocently by the punch bowl — pretending he knew nothing about the golden apple at all — quickly refused. "How can I judge my own wife?" he said. "Ask a mortal man instead. Ask Paris, the prince of Troy. He's the handsomest man on earth, so surely he can decide."

Paris was lying happily on a mountainside when the three goddesses suddenly appeared and demanded that he judge them. Each one tried to bribe him: Hera whispered that she would make him king of the world; Athena hissed that she would give him victory in every battle; and Aphrodite promised him the most beautiful woman on earth. Paris's eyes lit up, and he handed the apple straight to Aphrodite. Hera and Athena stormed back to Olympus, grumbling and complaining — and neither goddess would ever forget the prince who had rejected them.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past Simple — Narrating a Story
We use the past simple tense to tell a story about finished actions in the past.
Zeus looked down over Greece and saw men swarming like ants.
He thought and thought, and finally he came up with a plan.
Paris gave the golden apple to Aphrodite.
Tip: Notice the irregular past tense verbs: saw (see), thought (think), came (come), gave (give).
📌 Direct Speech — Quotation Marks
We use quotation marks (" ") to show the exact words a person said.
Zeus said, "There are too many people on the earth."
Hera snapped, "It's mine!"
Zeus wrote on the apple: "For the Most Beautiful."
Tip: A comma usually comes before the quotation mark when introducing speech: Zeus said, "..."
📌 Superlatives — '-est' and 'most'
We use superlatives to compare three or more things and say which is the extreme.
Each goddess believed she was the most beautiful of them all.
Paris was known as the handsomest man on earth.
Zeus was the chief — the most powerful — of all the gods.
Tip: Short adjectives usually add -est (handsomest); longer adjectives use 'most' (most beautiful).

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🌩️
Power without limits can be dangerous. Zeus had enormous power and used it to create a plan that would eventually cause a terrible war. Do you think leaders with unlimited power are more likely to make harmful decisions?
🍎
Small actions can cause enormous consequences. One tiny golden apple, tossed onto a floor, would eventually lead to a famous war. Can you think of a small event in history (or your own life) that led to huge results?
🎭
Leaders sometimes avoid responsibility for problems they create. Zeus created the entire situation, then pretended to know nothing and passed the decision to someone else. Have you ever seen someone avoid taking responsibility for a problem they caused?
💭
People often choose what they want personally over what benefits everyone. Paris chose the gift that pleased him personally, without thinking about how his choice would affect his country. Do people often make decisions this way in real life?
