2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🏛️

⛵ Do you remember the Etruscans? They were the tribes who lived in the north of Italy, near Rome. The Etruscans were great traders. They sailed across the sea to Greece to buy and sell pots, metal, and jewelry.

📜 In Greece, the Etruscans did not only trade. They also listened. They heard the Greek myths — exciting stories about gods, heroes, monsters, and wars. They learned the names of the Greek gods who lived on Mount Olympus. When they came home to Italy, they passed these stories along to their neighbors, the Romans.

🙏 The Romans loved the stories. Soon they took the Greek gods as their own. They worshipped the same gods and built temples for them. But there was one big change. The Romans gave the Greek gods new, Roman names.

The Romans borrowed the Greek gods, but they gave each god a new Roman name.

⚡ Do you remember Zeus, the king of the Greek gods? He made a golden apple so that he could start the Trojan War. In Rome, Zeus was called Jupiter. Jupiter controlled the sky, the moon, and the weather. When the wind blew hard, when rain fell, and when thunder crashed over the hills, the Romans said, "Jupiter is busy today!"

🪐 Today, the biggest planet in our solar system is called Jupiter. It is named after the king of the Roman gods. That makes sense, because Jupiter is the king of the planets too!

🔴 The red planet, Mars, also has a Roman name. Mars was the Roman god of war. The Romans were a nation of soldiers, so Mars was very important to them. Before a battle, Roman soldiers prayed to Mars and asked him to make them strong.

🌊 And do you remember Poseidon, the god of the sea? He was angry with Odysseus and tried to stop him from getting home. The Romans called this sea god Neptune. There is a blue planet far, far away from the sun that is named Neptune, too.

🌾 Why did the Romans tell so many stories about their gods? One reason is that the stories helped them explain the natural world. Why does it rain? Why does the sea become wild? Why do the leaves fall in autumn and grow again in spring? People in the ancient world did not have science books. So they told stories.

❄️ One of the most famous Roman stories tries to explain why we have winter and summer every year. It is about a goddess of the harvest named Ceres and her beautiful daughter, Proserpine. In the next lesson, you will hear what happened to them one day in the woods.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Passive: 'was called' / 'is named after'
Use was/is + past participle when the name is more important than who gave it.
In Rome, Zeus was called Jupiter.
The planet Mars is named after the god of war.
The sea god was called Neptune.
The biggest planet is named after the king of the gods.
💡 Tip: 'Named after' means 'given the same name as'.
📌 Past simple: regular and irregular verbs
Many past verbs end in -ed, but some important ones change completely.
The Etruscans learned the Greek myths.
They heard stories about gods. (hear → heard)
The Romans took the gods as their own. (take → took)
Jupiter controlled the weather.
💡 Tip: Learn irregular verbs in groups: hear–heard, take–took, tell–told.
📌 'so that' for purpose
Use 'so that' + subject + could/would to explain why someone did something.
Zeus made a golden apple so that he could start a war.
The Romans told stories so that they could explain the world.
Soldiers prayed to Mars so that he would make them strong.
💡 Tip: 'So that' answers the question 'Why?'

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🔄
Borrowing culture. The Romans borrowed Greek gods and stories. What things has your country borrowed from other cultures — food, words, festivals?
🌦️
Stories and science. Ancient people used myths to explain rain and thunder. How do we explain these things today? Are old stories still useful?
🪐
Names that last. The planets still carry Roman names after 2,000 years. Why do some names last so long?
🙏
Gods for everything. The Romans had a god for the sky, the sea, and war. Why do you think people wanted a god for each part of life?
