2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🏔️

After many days lost at sea, Odysseus and his tired men saw a beautiful island. It was full of wheat, grapevines, and wild goats. They landed their ships, hunted for food, and feasted. The next morning, they went to explore.

On the far side of the island, they found a huge cave carved into a cliff. In front of it stood a stone pen filled with hundreds of sheep and goats. "Who lives here?" Odysseus asked. "Let's go in and find out." He took twelve of his strongest men and a jug of sweet wine as a gift.

Carefully, they crept into the cave. Inside they found racks of cheese, each one bigger than a man's head, and bowls large enough to lie down in. "A giant lives here!" the men whispered. "Let's grab some food and run before he comes back!" But Odysseus refused. "We will wait and greet him," he said.

When night fell, they heard footsteps that shook the ground. In came the cave's owner. He was as tall as three men, and he had only one eye, right in the centre of his forehead. He was a Cyclops!

The Cyclops began to herd his sheep inside. Then he rolled a huge stone across the door — a stone so heavy that twenty men could not have moved it. The men were terrified. When the giant saw them, he roared, "Who are you? Robbers?" Odysseus begged for kindness and hospitality, saying they were only travellers.

"Where is your ship?" asked the Cyclops. Odysseus was afraid the giant would destroy it, so he lied. "It was lost in a shipwreck," he said. The Cyclops gave no answer. Instead, he grabbed two of the men and ate them on the spot! Then he lay down and fell asleep. Odysseus and his men were trapped, deep inside the cave, with a monster.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past Continuous vs. Past Simple
Use the past continuous for a longer action, and the past simple for a shorter action that interrupts it.
The Cyclops was herding his sheep when he saw the men.
The men were hiding when the giant lit the fire.
Odysseus was speaking when the Cyclops grabbed two men.
Tip: Pattern: was/were + -ing (long action) + when + past simple (short action).
📌 too + adjective
We use 'too' to say something is more than we want or can manage.
The stone was too heavy to move.
The cave was too dark to see clearly.
The giant was too strong to fight.
Tip: Pattern: too + adjective (+ to + verb). 'Too' always means a problem.
📌 Adverbs of manner
Adverbs tell us how someone does something. Many end in '-ly'.
They crept in carefully.
The men whispered quietly.
The Cyclops roared angrily.
Tip: Make an adverb by adding -ly to an adjective (careful → carefully).

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🤔
Curiosity can be dangerous. Odysseus stayed because he wanted to meet the cave's owner. When is curiosity a good thing, and when can it lead to trouble?
🚪
Guests and hosts have rules. Odysseus expected kindness from the Cyclops. Why do you think many cultures have special rules for how to treat guests?
🗣️
Sometimes leaders and followers disagree. The men wanted to run, but Odysseus made them stay. Should a leader always listen to the group, or sometimes decide alone?
🎭
A monster can teach us about fear. Stories about giants have been told for thousands of years. Why do you think humans enjoy scary stories about monsters?

💡 Big Idea

Lost at sea, Odysseus and his men entered the cave of the Cyclops, a one-eyed giant. The giant trapped them behind a huge stone and ate two of the men. Odysseus, thinking fast, lied about the ship to keep it safe. When we are trapped by a problem, panic rarely helps — but a calm, clever mind can find a way out.
