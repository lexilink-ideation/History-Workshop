2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🏛️

With the Persians finally defeated, Athens and Sparta no longer had to spend all their energy on war. Instead, the Greeks turned their creativity toward architecture — the art of designing beautiful buildings. They built enormous temples from marble, including the magnificent Parthenon, a temple built in honour of Athena, whose ruins still stand in Athens today.

Inside the Parthenon, artists carved stunning friezes — decorative pictures — including one showing a legendary battle between Greeks and centaurs, imaginary creatures that were half man and half horse. These carvings were remarkably realistic, showing muscles, expressions, and even the folds of clothing.

But this golden age of peace did not last. A growing fear crept back into both cities: Sparta worried Athens was becoming too powerful, and Athens worried about Sparta's fierce army. Instead of remaining friendly rivals, they fought each other again in the Peloponnesian War, which began in 431 BC and lasted an incredible 25 years.

Sparta marched to besiege Athens, but instead of meeting the Spartan army in open battle, the Athenians decided to hide behind their famous Long Walls and simply wait out the prolonged siege. For a while, this clever plan seemed to be working.

But then disaster struck: a deadly plague, spread by fleas that lived on rats, broke out inside the crowded city. People began to panic as the disease killed even Pericles, Athens's greatest general. Desperate, an ambitious Athenian named Alcibiades led a failed attack against Sparta, and when the furious Athenians turned against him, he deserted the city and became a traitor — leading the Spartan army through a secret passageway to capture Athens.

Sparta became the strongest city in Greece, but there was no true winner. By the time the war finally ended, both Athens and Sparta were completely exhausted, having lost countless soldiers over decades of fighting. Greece as a whole had grown dangerously vulnerable — with no strength left to defend itself against whatever invader might come next.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 'Rather than' — Contrasting Choices
We use 'rather than + verb-ing' to show a choice between two actions.
Rather than fighting in open battle, Athens hid behind its walls.
Rather than remaining friends, Sparta and Athens became rivals again.
Rather than attacking immediately, Sparta chose to besiege the city.
Tip: 'Rather than' is followed by an -ing form or a noun, and shows a preferred alternative.
📌 'If' Clauses — Imagining a Different Past
We use 'if' clauses to imagine how the past might have been different (unreal past conditional).
If the plague had not broken out, Athens's strategy might have worked.
If Alcibiades had not attacked, the Athenians might have survived longer.
Greece would have been stronger if the two cities had not fought each other.
Tip: This advanced structure (If + past perfect, would have + past participle) imagines alternate outcomes — useful for IELTS discussion.
📌 'No true winner' — Balanced Conclusions
We use balanced language to show that an outcome was not simply good or bad for one side.
There was no true winner in this long war.
Both Athens and Sparta were left exhausted.
Neither city had the strength left to defend itself.
Tip: This kind of balanced language is excellent for IELTS essays that discuss both sides of an argument.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🏛️
Peace often allows art and culture to flourish. The Greeks were only able to build the Parthenon once the Persian threat had ended. Do you think periods of peace are usually more creative and productive than periods of war?
😨
Fear of a rival's growing power can restart old conflicts. Even after uniting against Persia, Sparta and Athens turned on each other out of fear. Can you think of modern examples where fear of a rival's growing power leads to conflict?
🦠
Disease has changed the course of history many times. A plague, not a battle, was one of the biggest reasons Athens lost the war. Can you think of other moments in history where disease changed the outcome of a war or empire?
⚖️
Long wars can leave winners just as weakened as losers. Although Sparta 'won' the war, both cities were left exhausted and vulnerable. Do you think there is ever a true 'winner' in a long, costly war?

💡 Big Idea

With peace restored, the Greeks built the magnificent Parthenon — but fear of each other's power soon dragged Athens and Sparta into a 25-year war that left both cities plague-stricken, betrayed, and exhausted. Even the greatest civilisations can be weakened not by outside enemies, but by their own internal rivalries.
