2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
⛰️

The years passed. Romulus and Remus grew up to be strong young men. One day they walked back to the fig tree where their basket had landed so many years ago. They looked around and saw seven hills. "This is the perfect place to build a town," they said. "A town on top of these hills would be strong and hard to attack!"

🧱 So the brothers began to build. But who would be in charge? Romulus declared himself the king of the new town. He put himself in charge of building a wall around it. "This wall will keep us safe!" he said proudly. "Anyone who climbs over my wall will be killed at once!"

"This wall will keep us safe! Anyone who climbs over my wall will instantly be killed!"

😠 Remus was angry. He thought, "We built this town together! Why should Romulus be the ruler of it? I want to be the leader too." His anger grew and grew. He decided to show his brother that the wall was not so strong.

🦘 Remus walked up to the wall and vaulted easily over it. Then he turned around and sneered, "What kind of a wall is that? Anyone can climb over it! How can you keep this town safe?"

⚔️ Romulus was furious. He drew his sword, charged at his brother, and killed him on the spot. It was a terrible moment, and the town had not even been named yet. The two brothers who had survived the river and the wolf now had a deadly quarrel.

🏛️ Romulus named the town Rome, after himself. He became Rome's first king. Whether the story is true or not, it shows how the Romans thought about their past. They believed their city began with courage, but also with blood and the hunger for power.

📚 Does this story remind you of anything? Do you remember Sargon, the king who floated down a river in a basket until someone saved him? And do you remember Cyrus, who was raised by shepherds in the woods? Ancient people loved stories like these. They made their kings seem even more legendary, like fairy-tale heroes who could do anything!

🕰️ Today, Romans still celebrate the birthday of their city in April. Visitors can walk on the hills where the legend says the town began. Many of the seven hills are covered with ruins, parks, and old churches. The wall of Romulus is long gone, but the story of the brothers is still told to children all over Italy.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Reported speech with quotes
Use quotation marks for the exact words people say.
"This is the perfect place," they said.
"What kind of wall is that?" he sneered.
"This wall will keep us safe!" he declared.
💡 Tip: Use verbs like said, declared, sneered to show the speaker's feeling.
📌 Modal 'will' for promises and threats
Use 'will' for strong statements about the future.
This wall will keep us safe.
Anyone who climbs over will be killed.
A town on the hills would be hard to attack.
💡 Tip: 'Will' is stronger than 'might'.
📌 Reflexive: himself
Use 'himself' when the subject and object are the same person.
Romulus declared himself king.
He named the town after himself.
He put himself in charge.
💡 Tip: himself/herself/themselves for the same subject.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🧱
Walls and safety. Romulus believed a wall would keep the town safe. Do walls (real or invisible) really protect us?
😡
Pride and anger. Remus mocked his brother and paid a terrible price. How can we avoid hurting people with our words?
🤝
Sharing leadership. The twins built the town together, but only one could rule. Is it possible to share leadership fairly?
📜
How legends shape identity. The Romans were proud of this violent story. Why do countries sometimes tell proud stories about their dark beginnings?
