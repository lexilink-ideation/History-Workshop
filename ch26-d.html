2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🗿

Think about the Olmec temple on top of the great clay pyramid. Long ago, the temple disappeared. But the statues that stood around it are still here! They are not statues of whole people. They are only statues of heads.

🪨 Experts think the Olmec heads are sculptures of important rulers. But none of these stone rulers has a body. Each head sits directly on the ground, as if an enormous stone person had been buried in the dirt up to his neck. It looks very strange!

📏 How big are they? Some are nine feet tall! That is taller than the biggest person you know, and probably higher than the ceiling of your room. The eyes are bigger than your whole head. You could put your entire hand up one of the noses! If you stood next to a head, your own head would only reach its cheek.

If the stone heads had bodies, they would be giants as tall as a four-story building.

🏢 Imagine it. If these heads had bodies, they would be stone giants, as tall as a four-story building. Their hands would be big enough for you to sit in. A grown-up would not even reach their knees!

⭕ The huge heads sat in a circle around the temple on top of the pyramid. So what were they for? Nobody really knows. Perhaps the Olmecs, like the Egyptians, thought that their rulers were gods and wanted to honor them. Maybe the Olmecs carved the heads to remember their rulers. We do the same thing today when we name airports, roads, and buildings after our leaders.

💭 Some archaeologists have a different idea. They say the heads may have been altars where people left gifts for the gods. Others think the heads were thrones for the kings. A ruler could sit on the stone and look very powerful.

🔐 We will never know the answer for sure. Like the Nazca drawings, the Olmec heads will stay mysterious. Even so, they teach us something. The Olmecs were clever, strong, and well organized. They could move heavy stones for miles and carve them with great skill.

🌎 Today, some of the heads can be seen in museums and parks in Mexico. Visitors stand next to them and feel very small. Many people take photographs of the strong faces, with their wide noses and thick lips. Each face looks a little different, so scientists believe every head showed a real ruler. Imagine the rulers who sat in this circle, looking out over the city, long before any books were written in the Americas.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 'If' sentences about imaginary things
Use 'if + past, would + verb' for things that are not real.
If the heads had bodies, they would be giants.
If you stood next to one, your head would reach its cheek.
If you were an Olmec, you would want to be rich.
💡 Tip: After 'if', use the past tense. After 'would', use the base verb.
📌 Measurements and 'as ... as'
Use 'as + adjective + as' to compare size.
Some heads are as tall as nine feet.
They would be as tall as a four-story building.
The eyes are bigger than your whole head.
💡 Tip: 'As ... as' means equal. 'Bigger than' means more.
📌 'Perhaps', 'maybe', 'some think'
Use these to share different ideas when you are not sure.
Perhaps the Olmecs wanted to honor their rulers.
Maybe they carved the heads to remember them.
Some archaeologists think they were altars.
💡 Tip: Use 'perhaps/maybe' at the start of a sentence.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🗿
Why remember rulers? The Olmecs carved giant heads of rulers. Who deserves a statue in your country, and why?
🏗️
Moving giant stones. Olmec workers moved huge stones without modern machines. What does this tell you about teamwork?
❓
Living with mysteries. We will never know why the heads were made. Is it okay not to know everything about the past?
👁️
Faces in art. The heads have huge eyes and noses. Why do artists often make important people very large?
