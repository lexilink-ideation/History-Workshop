The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🌊

The Mycenaeans are gone. Now Greece is full of barbarians — the Dorians from the north and the Sea People from across the Mediterranean. These people cannot read or write. They are not used to living in cities. It looks like Greek civilisation has ended!

But wait — something strange is happening. The longer these barbarians live in Greece, the more civilised they become. They stop wandering around looking for cities to attack. Instead, they settle down. They learn how to farm and fish. Little by little, they are becoming — Greeks!

The Dorians and the Sea People lived in Greece for hundreds of years. They looked less and less like wild warriors, and more and more like merchants, farmers, and storekeepers.

They started building houses. Their houses became fancier and fancier, with kitchens, rooms for baths, and separate rooms for men and women. Soon they built houses close together in villages. The villages grew into cities — each one with its own government and its own army. They learned to grow olives, grapes, figs, and wheat, and to make wine from the grapes.

Instead of riding out to kill their neighbours, they learned to enjoy peaceful sports like wrestling, chariot racing, and horseback riding. They loved to dance — at weddings, at funerals, at feasts, and at sports events. In fact, they soon invented over two hundred different dances!

The rich women no longer went out to look for food. They stayed indoors, away from the sun, so their skin would stay smooth and pale. They kept slaves to do the hard work. Now these people were known, simply, as Greeks. Very soon, they would do something that changed the world forever: they would learn to read and write, and to tell the stories of their ancestors.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past Simple — Regular & Irregular
We use the past simple to describe finished actions in history.
BasePastFrom the story
learnlearnedThey learned how to farm and fish.
buildbuiltThey built houses close together.
growgrewThe villages grew into cities.
becomebecameThe barbarians became Greeks.
Tip: Regular verbs add -ed (learn → learned). Irregular verbs change their form (grow → grew) and must be memorised.
📌 Comparatives — '-er' and 'more'
We use comparatives to show how something changes or how two things differ.
Their houses became fancier and fancier.
The villages grew larger than before.
They looked more and more like civilised merchants.
Tip: Short adjectives add -er (large → larger). Long adjectives use more (more civilised). Repeat the word ('larger and larger') to show a slow change.
📌 'instead of' + noun / -ing
We use 'instead of' to show that one thing replaces another.
Instead of wandering, they settled down.
They became merchants instead of warriors.
Instead of attacking their neighbours, they held sports.
Tip: After instead of we use a noun (warriors) or an -ing verb (wandering).

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🏘️
Living together changes people. When the tribes stopped wandering and lived in villages, they became more peaceful. Why do you think sharing a place with others changes how people behave?
⚔️
Warriors became farmers. The same people who once attacked cities later grew olives and grapes. Can a person or a group completely change who they are? What might cause such a change?
🕊️
Peace allowed art and sport to grow. Only when the fighting stopped did the Greeks invent dances and games. Why do you think peace helps culture grow?
👥
Every civilisation keeps some unfair rules. The Greeks kept slaves and did not treat women equally. Why is it important to look honestly at the mistakes of great civilisations?

💡 Big Idea

When the Mycenaeans fell, wild tribes moved into Greece. But over hundreds of years, they settled down, built cities, and became civilised Greeks. A great civilisation is not born in a day — it grows slowly, step by step, when people choose to live and build together.
