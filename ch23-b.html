2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
⚡

Now that you have met Zeus, Hera, Athena, and Aphrodite, it is worth pausing the story to properly introduce the whole family of Greek gods. The Greeks believed in twelve great Olympians, each with their own special domain — a particular area of life they controlled.

Zeus ruled the sky and carried a mighty thunderbolt as his weapon; Hera, his wife, watched over marriage and family. Athena, born fully grown from Zeus's head, was the goddess of wisdom as well as war. Aphrodite ruled over love and beauty, while Poseidon, Zeus's brother, commanded the sea. Hades, another brother, ruled the shadowy underworld beneath the earth. Other Olympians included Apollo, god of the sun and music; Artemis, goddess of the hunt and the moon; Ares, the fierce god of war; Hermes, the swift messenger god; and Demeter, goddess of the harvest, who made crops grow.

With the whole family in mind, let's return to our story. Aphrodite kept her promise to Paris without delay. She travelled to the palace of Helen — the most beautiful woman in the world — and made Helen fall in love with Paris the moment she saw him. Helen ran away with Paris to live with him in Troy.

Unfortunately, Helen was already married — to Menelaus, the king of the Greeks! When Menelaus discovered that his wife had vanished, he was absolutely furious, feeling completely betrayed by both his wife and the prince of Troy. He called on the gods and on every Greek soldier he could find to help him fight against Troy, defeat Paris, and win Helen back.

This is where the gods' different powers and personalities truly came into play. Hera, still resenting Paris for rejecting her at the judgment, chose to fight against Troy, and Athena, goddess of war, joined her as an ally. Aphrodite, of course, remained loyal to Paris and decided to side with Troy. Apollo, the sun god, also supported Troy, while Poseidon, god of the sea, wanted to see the city completely destroyed and joined the other side.

One by one, the Olympians lined up for or against Troy — not because of who was right or wrong, but because of old grudges, personal loyalty, and wounded pride. And so the Trojan War began, lasting for years and years of bloodshed and death. If you trace this entire war back to its true beginning, you arrive at something surprisingly small: a single golden apple. This myth's clearest lesson is that the root cause of even the greatest conflicts can be a tiny, personal choice.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Passive Voice — 'was/were + past participle'
We use the passive voice when the action matters more than who did it.
Helen was married to Menelaus.
The war was fought because of a single golden apple.
A queen's heart was changed forever by Aphrodite's magic.
Tip: Passive voice: be (was/were) + past participle. Useful for describing what happened TO someone.
📌 Contrast Linkers — 'while' and 'whereas'
We use 'while' and 'whereas' to compare two very different situations.
While Hera fought against Troy, Aphrodite fought for it.
Athena sided against Paris, whereas Aphrodite remained loyal to him.
Zeus ruled the sky, while Poseidon ruled the sea.
Tip: 'While' and 'whereas' highlight a clear contrast between two clauses in the same sentence.
📌 'All because of' — Emphasising a Small Cause
We use 'all because of + noun' to emphasise that something surprisingly small caused a huge result.
The war began all because of a single golden apple.
Years of bloodshed happened all because of one choice.
Two great civilisations went to war all because of a wedding gift.
Tip: This phrase emphasises surprise — that something so small could cause something so large.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🏳️
Even powerful beings can act selfishly in conflicts. The gods didn't choose sides based on justice — they chose based on personal feelings. Do you think even the most powerful leaders today sometimes act this way?
⚡
Every culture invents gods to explain the forces of nature and life. The Greeks gave each god a specific power — the sky, the sea, love, wisdom. Why do you think ancient people organised their gods this way?
🌍
Conflicts often grow bigger as more people or groups get involved. What began as an argument between three goddesses grew to involve gods, kings, entire armies, and years of war. Why do conflicts often expand this way?
⚖️
Was anyone in this story truly 'right'? Looking at the whole story — Zeus, Eris, the three goddesses, Paris, Helen, and Menelaus — do you think any single person or god was completely blameless? Why or why not?

💡 Big Idea

As the twelve Olympians revealed their unique powers, old grudges pushed them into opposing camps, dragging both gods and mortals into the Trojan War. Conflicts often grow because everyone involved — even the powerful — chooses sides for personal reasons.
