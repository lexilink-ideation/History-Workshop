2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🪓

The Etruscan kings carried a special object everywhere they went. It was a bundle of wooden rods tied together, with an axe blade pushed into the middle. At first, this might look strange. Why would a king carry sticks and an axe? But the object had a clear meaning. It was a symbol of royal power.

⚖️ Each part of the symbol told people something. The rods showed that the king had the power to punish anyone who did wrong. The axe blade showed that he could execute people who did very evil things. Everyone who saw the bundle understood: this king is in charge, and he can punish you.

The rods showed the king could punish wrongdoers, and the axe showed he could execute people who did very evil things.

🏛️ The Romans liked this symbol very much. They called it the fasces. Soon the Roman kings, like the Etruscan kings, wore purple-bordered togas and carried the fasces. When a king walked through the streets, his servants walked in front of him with the fasces so that everyone would make way.

💰 Do you have an old dime? Look on the back, and you may see a picture of the fasces! The symbol did not stay in ancient Rome. American courtrooms and government offices sometimes copy it, even today. They put a fasces in the room to show that the judges have the power to punish criminals.

🇺🇸 The courtroom of the United States Capitol building has two fasces on the wall, one on each side of the American flag. If you visit, you may see them yourself. Thousands of years after Rome's first kings, people still remember their symbol.

🤔 Symbols are powerful because they tell a story without words. You see a red cross, and you think of a hospital. You see a flag, and you think of a country. The fasces told Romans something important: powerful rulers could use force. But as you will see in the next lesson, the people of Rome did not want to let one ruler have that much power forever.

🌟 History is full of ideas that travel across time. A tool of the Etruscan kings became a symbol of Rome, and a symbol of Rome became part of the buildings of a modern country. Ideas can live much longer than the people who first had them.

🔍 Next time you visit a courthouse or look at an old coin, check carefully for the fasces. It is a small door to the ancient world.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Relative clauses with 'who' and 'that'
Use 'who' for people and 'that' for things.
The rods showed that the king could punish anyone who did wrong.
He could execute people who did very evil things.
It was a symbol that everyone understood.
💡 Tip: who = people, that/which = things.
📌 Present simple for things that are still true
Use the present simple for facts that are true now.
Courtrooms today have a fasces on the wall.
Judges have the power to punish.
You can see it on an old dime.
💡 Tip: Present simple shows a link between past and present.
📌 Imperatives for invitations: 'Look at...'
Use the base verb to invite the reader to do something.
Look on the back of an old dime.
Find the fasces on the wall.
Think about what the symbol means.
💡 Tip: No subject is needed in imperatives.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🪓
Power and fear. The fasces made people fear the king. Is it good for a ruler to use fear? Why or why not?
🏛️
Old symbols in new places. A Roman symbol appears in American courts. Why do modern countries copy ancient symbols?
🏴
What do symbols mean to you? Think about a flag, a logo, or a badge. What does it make you feel?
⚖️
Who should punish? The king could punish and execute alone. Why is it better for judges and laws to decide?
