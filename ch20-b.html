The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
✍️

We know much more about the early Greeks than about the wild barbarians before them. Why? Because the early Greeks soon learned how to read and write. They began to record stories about their ancestors. They wrote down the myths and fairy tales they told their children — and we still have some of that writing today.

The early Greeks did not use the same letters that we use now. They used their own alphabet. They probably learned some of these symbols from the Phoenicians, who had one of the very first alphabets in the world. The Greeks did not invent everything — they borrowed good ideas and made them their own.

Look at the first Greek letter. It is called an alpha. If you think it looks like an "a," you are right — and it sounds like an "a" too!

Next comes the beta, which makes the "b" sound. Put alpha and beta together and you get the word alphabet! There is also a kappa (our "k") and a tau (our "t"). Our modern letters A, B, E, H, I, K, M, N, O, P, T, X, Y, and Z all come from these ancient Greek symbols.

Some Greek letters look nothing like ours. One is called a psi. It makes a "ps" sound, which we find hard to pronounce in English. The psi looks a little like a trident — the three-pointed spear carried by Poseidon, the god of the sea. Can you hear the "ps" sound in Poseidon's name?

There is one more special letter, the theta, which makes the "th" sound. Even though some Greek letters are different from ours, we owe a great deal to the Greeks. Every time you write your name with an A, B, or K, you are using an ancient Greek letter — thousands of years old!

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Present Simple for facts that are still true
We use the present simple for facts that were true then and are still true now.
The letter alpha sounds like an 'a'.
Our alphabet comes from the ancient Greeks.
The psi makes a 'ps' sound.
Tip: Even in a history lesson, use the present simple for things that are always true today.
📌 Past Simple vs. still-true facts
Use the past for finished actions, but the present for facts that continue.
Finished (past)Still true (present)
The Greeks learned to write.We use Greek letters today.
They borrowed letters from the Phoenicians.Alpha looks like an 'a'.
Tip: Ask yourself: is the action finished, or is it still true right now?
📌 Relative clauses with 'who' and 'which'
We use 'who' for people and 'which' for things to add extra information.
The Phoenicians, who had the first alphabet, taught the Greeks.
Poseidon, who lived in the sea, carried a trident.
The psi, which makes a 'ps' sound, looks like a trident.
Tip: who = people; which = things or animals.

Ideas & Society

📝
Writing changes memory. Before writing, all stories lived only in people's heads. How do you think writing changed the way humans remember the past?
🔗
Good ideas travel between cultures. The Greeks borrowed the alphabet from the Phoenicians. Is it a good thing when one culture borrows ideas from another? Why or why not?

💡 Big Idea

The early Greeks learned to read and write, borrowing letters from the Phoenicians to create their own alphabet — alpha, beta, kappa, tau, and more. The alphabet was one of the greatest inventions in history: it let people record their stories and pass them down for thousands of years.
