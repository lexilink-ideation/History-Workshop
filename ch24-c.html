The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🏛️

Chapter 24 carried Greece through nearly a hundred years of history — from the terrifying Persian invasion, to two of the most famous battles in history at Marathon and Salamis, to a golden age of art and architecture, and finally into a long, exhausting civil war between Athens and Sparta.

Notice how the chapter's mood shifts: unity and courage against Persia, creativity and pride during the building of the Parthenon, and finally fear, disease, and betrayal during the Peloponnesian War.

As you move into Chapter 25, keep this pattern in mind: a divided, exhausted Greece was about to attract the attention of a new and ambitious neighbour to the north.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Review — Passive Voice in Historical Writing
This chapter used the passive voice frequently to describe major historical events.
The Parthenon was built in honour of Athena.
The Battle of Salamis was fought in 480 BC.
Athens was captured with the help of a traitor.
Tip: The passive voice (was/were + past participle) is essential for describing historical events clearly and formally.
📌 Review — Cause and Effect Across a Chapter
We tracked several major cause-and-effect chains across this entire chapter.
Persia's threat caused Athens and Sparta to become allies.
The plague led to Pericles's death and Athens's weakness.
Alcibiades's betrayal resulted in the fall of Athens.
Tip: Cause-and-effect language (caused, led to, resulted in) is essential for explaining complex historical chains of events.
📌 Review — Balanced Contrast for Essays
We practised showing two sides of a situation using balanced contrast language.
While Sparta won the war, both sides were left exhausted.
Athens was powerful in art and ideas, whereas Sparta was powerful in war.
Although Persia was defeated, a new conflict soon began.
Tip: Balanced contrast language (while, whereas, although) is a key IELTS writing skill for discussing two sides of an issue.

📜 Chapter 24 Summary

In Chapter 24, we followed nearly a century of Greek history, beginning with the Persian Empire's attempt to conquer Greece around 500 BC. Facing this shared threat, rivals Athens and Sparta set aside their old quarrel and became allies. At the Battle of Marathon in 490 BC, badly outnumbered Athenian soldiers defeated the Persian army, and the legendary runner Pheidippides inspired the modern marathon race. The war continued until the decisive naval Battle of Salamis in 480 BC, where clever Greek strategy defeated a much larger Persian fleet, finally ending the Persian threat for good. With peace restored, the Greeks turned to art and architecture, constructing the magnificent marble Parthenon in honour of Athena, decorated with realistic friezes depicting legendary battles. But peace between the Greek cities did not last. Fear of each other's growing power dragged Athens and Sparta into the 25-year Peloponnesian War in 431 BC. Athens hid behind its famous Long Walls, but a devastating plague broke out inside the crowded city, killing the great general Pericles. Finally, the ambitious Athenian Alcibiades betrayed his own city, leading Spartan soldiers through a secret passageway to capture Athens. By the end of the chapter, Sparta had become the most powerful city in Greece — but decades of war had left the entire region exhausted and dangerously vulnerable to any future invader.

💡 Big Idea

From uniting against Persia to tearing each other apart in the Peloponnesian War, the Greek city-states showed both remarkable courage and painful self-destruction. Even the greatest civilisations can be weakened not by outside enemies, but by their own internal rivalries.
