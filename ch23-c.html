The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🏛️

Chapter 23 told the story of how the Greek gods, who the ancient Greeks believed watched over human life from Mount Olympus, became tangled up in a very human kind of quarrel: jealousy over who was "the most beautiful."

From Zeus's mischievous golden apple, to the twelve Olympians and their powers, to Paris's fateful choice, to Helen's flight to Troy, every event connected to the next — until an entire war, involving both gods and mortals, had begun.

As you move into Chapter 24, keep this lesson in mind: history and mythology are full of enormous events that trace back to small, very human decisions — pride, jealousy, love, and revenge.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Review — Past Simple for Narrating History
Throughout this chapter, we used the past simple to narrate a chain of historical and mythical events.
Zeus made a golden apple and gave it to Eris.
Paris chose Aphrodite's gift over the other two offers.
Menelaus called on the gods and Greek soldiers to help him.
Tip: The past simple is the backbone of storytelling: it moves the narrative forward, one event at a time.
📌 Review — Cause and Effect Linkers
We practised many linkers that show cause and effect across this chapter's story.
The war began all because of a single golden apple.
As soon as Helen saw Paris, she fell in love with him.
If you trace the war back, you find one small choice.
Tip: Cause-and-effect language (because of, as soon as, trace back) is essential for explaining WHY things happen in history.
📌 Review — Contrast Linkers
We used contrast linkers to compare different characters' choices and feelings.
While Hera fought against Troy, Aphrodite fought for it.
Some gods chose sides because of loyalty, whereas others chose out of anger.
The wedding seemed joyful, but it hid a dangerous plan.
Tip: Contrast linkers (while, whereas, but) help writers compare and contrast ideas clearly — an essential IELTS skill.

📜 Chapter 23 Summary

In Chapter 23, we explored the world of Greek mythology, beginning with the idea that the ancient Greeks were polytheists who believed a whole family of twelve Olympian gods lived on Mount Olympus, each with their own domain — Zeus over the sky, Poseidon over the sea, Athena over wisdom and war, Aphrodite over love, and more. The chief god, Zeus, grew tired of the crowded earth and created a golden apple marked "For the Most Beautiful," handing it to Eris, the goddess of strife, to drop at a wedding. The apple sparked a furious argument between Hera, Athena, and Aphrodite, and Zeus cleverly avoided judging them himself, instead sending the decision to a mortal prince named Paris. Bribed with the promise of the most beautiful woman on earth, Paris chose Aphrodite — permanently angering Hera and Athena. Aphrodite then made Helen, already married to King Menelaus of the Greeks, fall hopelessly in love with Paris, and Helen ran away with him to Troy. Furious and betrayed, Menelaus called on Greek warriors — and the gods themselves — to help him fight against Troy. The Olympians split into allies for and against the city based on old grudges and personal loyalty, and the war that followed, the legendary Trojan War, lasted for years and brought great bloodshed. By the end of the chapter, one clear lesson stands out: a single small choice, like tossing a golden apple onto a wedding floor, can grow into consequences far larger than anyone could ever imagine.

💡 Big Idea

A single golden apple, tossed at a wedding out of boredom and mischief, grew step by step into one of history's most legendary wars. The smallest choices — a gift, a bribe, a broken promise — can shape the fate of nations.
