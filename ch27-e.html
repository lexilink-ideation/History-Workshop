2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
👑

The legend says that Romulus was the first of seven kings of Rome. These kings wanted Rome to become bigger and stronger. So they fought with other tribes who lived in Italy. They wanted to take over more and more land.

🏔️ The most important of these tribes was the Etruscans. They lived north of Rome, in the hills and mountains of Italy. The Etruscans were clever and creative. They loved music and art, and they painted pictures that we can still see today. They also grew crops and made weapons and jewelry out of metal.

⛵ The Etruscans were also sailors. They went back and forth between Greece and Italy, where they traded with the Greeks. On these trips, they learned many new things. They learned to use the Greek alphabet. They learned to worship the Greek gods.

The Roman kings fought with the Etruscans, but they also traded with them and learned from them.

🤝 The Roman kings did something clever. They fought with the Etruscans, but they also traded with them. Instead of only seeing the Etruscans as enemies, the Romans watched and learned. The Etruscans taught the Romans how to dress like the Greeks. They told the Romans about the Greek gods. The Romans learned painting and music from the Etruscans too.

👘 The Romans also borrowed the customs of the Etruscan kings. These kings wore special robes called togas, with purple borders. The purple color showed everyone how important the king was. Soon the Roman kings were wearing purple-bordered togas as well!

🌍 This tells us something about Rome. The Romans did not invent everything themselves. They were very good at borrowing the best ideas from other people and using them in new ways. Later, they would do the same with the Greeks, and with many other peoples they met.

💡 Learning from your neighbors can be a great strength. A small village in the hills became powerful partly because it was willing to learn. Perhaps this is one reason why Rome grew so strong.

🎶 Imagine a Roman child watching an Etruscan musician play a flute. The music sounds new and beautiful. Imagine the child looking at a wall painting full of dancers and horses. Moments like these slowly changed Rome. The village in the hills became a city of art and ideas, as well as a city of soldiers.

🧵 Even small things changed. A Roman family might start to hang a painted cloth on the wall, or learn a new song for a festival. Over many years, such small changes added up.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past simple and 'used to'
Use 'used to' for habits and things that were true in the past.
The Etruscans used to sail between Greece and Italy.
Kings used to wear purple-bordered togas.
The Romans used to have kings.
💡 Tip: Used to + base verb.
📌 Verbs followed by -ing: 'liked', 'learned how to'
Some verbs are followed by -ing or 'to + verb'.
They liked painting pictures.
The Etruscans learned how to use the Greek alphabet.
They wanted to take over more land.
💡 Tip: like + -ing, want + to + verb.
📌 Contrast with 'but also'
'But also' adds a second, surprising idea.
The Romans fought the Etruscans but also traded with them.
They were warriors, but also artists.
He was a king, but also a leader.
💡 Tip: Often used after 'not only' or after a first positive idea.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🎨
Learning from neighbors. The Romans learned art, music, and religion from the Etruscans. What have you learned from your neighbors or friends from other cultures?
⚔️
Fight and trade. Rome both fought and traded with the Etruscans. Can countries be rivals and partners at the same time?
💜
Colors of power. Purple showed that the king was important. What colors or clothes show power or status in the modern world?
✈️
Borrowing in the modern world. Today, countries borrow ideas such as technology and food from each other. Is that mostly good or bad?
