The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🔥

The next evening, the Cyclops ate two more men. But Odysseus was ready. "Cyclops," he said, "you must be thirsty. Here is the best sweet wine you have ever tasted." The giant drank the whole jug and grew very sleepy. "What is your name?" he growled. "My name is Noman," said Odysseus. "Then I will eat Noman last!" laughed the giant, and he fell asleep.

Now Odysseus put his clever plan into action. Earlier, he had cut a long log and helped his men sharpen the end, hardening the point in the fire. With great courage, four men lifted the burning log and drove it straight into the giant's single eye. This was their bold trick to blind him — for a blind giant could not catch them.

The Cyclops leaped up and roared with pain! He grabbed blindly all around the cave, but the men slipped easily away from his hands.

His neighbours heard the noise and called out, "Who is hurting you?" "Noman! Noman is trying to kill me!" cried the Cyclops. "No man?" they answered. "Then go back to sleep!" And they all went away. Odysseus's clever name had fooled them all.

In the morning, the blind giant rolled away the stone to let his sheep out. But he felt the back of every animal, so no man could sneak past. So Odysseus tied the sheep together in threes, and each man held on to the thick fleece under the middle sheep. The Cyclops felt the sheep's backs — but never the men hiding below. In this way, they all managed to escape!

Safe on the ship, Odysseus could not stay quiet. "Cyclops! If anyone asks who blinded you, tell them it was Odysseus!" The furious giant tore a huge boulder from the cliff and hurled it at the ship. Then he shouted a terrible curse: "I am the son of Poseidon! I will ask him to sink your ship!" And Poseidon heard his son. He sent winds and waves that kept Odysseus lost for ten long years.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past Perfect — the earlier past
Use the past perfect (had + past participle) for an action that happened before another past action.
Odysseus put his plan to work — he had cut the log the day before.
By morning the men had escaped from the cave.
The giant ate the men Odysseus had warned about.
Tip: Pattern: had + past participle. It shows which past action came first.
📌 could / couldn't for ability
We use 'could' and 'couldn't' to talk about ability in the past.
A blind giant could not catch them.
The men could slip away in the dark.
The Cyclops could not feel the men under the sheep.
Tip: could = was able to; couldn't = was not able to.
📌 so + adjective + that
We use 'so...that' to show a strong result.
The wine was so strong that the giant fell asleep.
He roared so loudly that his neighbours woke up.
The boulder was so heavy that it made huge waves.
Tip: Pattern: so + adjective + that + result.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🧠
Brains can beat strength. Odysseus was far weaker than the giant, yet he won with a clever plan. Do you agree that intelligence is more powerful than force? Why?
🗣️
Pride can be dangerous. Odysseus could not resist shouting his name — and it cost him ten years. Why do you think it is sometimes wise to stay quiet?
⚖️
Was the trick fair? Odysseus tricked and blinded the giant to survive. Do you think his actions were right, since the Cyclops had eaten his men? Where is the line?
🌊
Actions have long consequences. One proud shout led to years of suffering. Can you think of a small action that led to very large results, in history or in life?

💡 Big Idea

Odysseus gave the Cyclops strong wine, told him his name was 'Noman,' and then blinded the sleeping giant with a sharpened log. The men escaped by hiding under the sheep. But when Odysseus proudly shouted his real name, the Cyclops cursed him, and Poseidon kept him lost for ten years. Cleverness can win the day — but pride can undo even the greatest victory.
