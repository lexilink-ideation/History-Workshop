2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🏛️

Let us think back over the great empires we have studied. Assyria was a great kingdom, but Babylon conquered it. Babylon was a great empire, but Persia conquered Babylon. Persia and Greece were great empires, but Alexander the Great conquered them. Alexander built the biggest kingdom of all. Then he died, and his generals broke the kingdom into pieces.

🔁 This is the pattern of the ancient world. One king comes along, wins battles, and builds a big empire. For a little while the empire is strong. But the kings who come after him slowly lose control. Then a king from another country does the same thing, and the cycle starts again. It happens over and over.

An empire grows when one strong ruler wins battles, and it falls when later rulers slowly lose control.

🌍 So it will not surprise you that a new empire is coming. But this one was bigger and stronger than any empire before it. It was even bigger than Alexander's! And it lasted much longer, for hundreds and hundreds of years.

📚 Here is something amazing. More than a thousand years after this empire fell, people were still learning its language, reading its books, and copying its government. This great empire was called Rome.

🏘️ But Rome did not begin as an empire. At first, it was just a tiny village in the hills of Italy. Find Greece on a map and go west. You will see a piece of land that looks like a boot. It sticks out into the Mediterranean Sea. We call this kind of land a peninsula, because it sticks out into the water. This peninsula is Italy.

⛰️ In the hills of this boot-shaped land, a handful of farmers and shepherds lived in small houses. They could not guess that their little village would one day rule the whole Mediterranean world. Nobody could.

📜 The people who lived in Rome told a legend about how their village began. A legend is a story from long ago. It may not be completely true, but it tells us what the people believed and loved. In the next lesson, you will hear the legend of two baby boys and a river.

🧠 As you read about Rome, keep one question in your mind: why did Rome last so long when so many empires fell quickly? Historians have many answers. Some say Rome had strong armies. Others say Rome learned from the people it met. Soon you will see how Rome borrowed ideas from its neighbors and made them its own.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past simple passive-like structure: 'was conquered by'
Use 'was/were + past participle + by' to say who did the action.
Assyria was conquered by Babylon.
Persia was conquered by Alexander.
The kingdom was broken up by his generals.
💡 Tip: The subject is the one that receives the action.
📌 Repeating patterns with 'over and over'
Use these phrases to show something happens again and again.
This happens over and over.
Kings win battles again and again.
The cycle starts all over again.
💡 Tip: Good for describing patterns in history.
📌 Comparing: bigger and stronger than
Use two comparatives to describe something that is better in two ways.
Rome was bigger and stronger than any empire before it.
It lasted much longer than Alexander's empire.
The empire was more powerful than Persia.
💡 Tip: Use 'than' to complete the comparison.
