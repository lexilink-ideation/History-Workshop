The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
😨

One night, King Astyages of Media had a terrible dream. He dreamed that his own baby grandson — the son of his daughter — grew up, took away his power, and became ruler of the Persians in his place. When Astyages woke up, he was frightened. He could not stop thinking about it.

"If I do not do something about this," he thought, "my grandson will throw me off my throne and take my crown for himself!" Astyages sat awake all night, worrying about his dream.

Astyages decided he could not take any chances. As soon as morning came, he called for his most trusted advisor, a man named Harpagus, and gave him a secret command.

When Harpagus arrived, Astyages did not waste any time. "I have a job for you," the king said. Harpagus, eager to please his king, answered at once. "I will do anything you tell me to, my king!"

Astyages leaned forward. "Good," he said. "Take my grandson out to the mountains, and get rid of him secretly. Do not let anyone know what you have done. Now go!"

Harpagus bowed and left the palace at once, carrying the baby in his arms. As he walked slowly out of the city and toward the wild mountains beyond, he could not stop thinking about what he had just been asked to do. The baby slept peacefully, completely unaware of the terrible fate that awaited him.

Harpagus was shocked by this terrible command. He did not want to hurt an innocent baby. But he also knew that if he chose to disobey the king, Astyages would surely punish him — and perhaps his whole family, too. Harpagus took the baby and walked slowly toward the mountains, his mind racing. Something inside him seemed to warn him that this was wrong. But could he really refuse a direct order from the king?

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Reported Speech — "told him to..."
We can report what someone said using "told + person + to + verb" instead of the exact words.
Astyages told Harpagus to take the baby to the mountains.
He told him to keep the plan secret.
Harpagus was told to get rid of the baby.
Tip: Pattern: told + person + to + base verb. This is a common way to report an order in English.
📌 First Conditional — "If... will..."
We use the first conditional to talk about a possible future result of an action.
"If I do not act, my grandson will take my throne," Astyages thought.
If Harpagus disobeys, the king will punish him.
If the plan works, no one will know the truth.
Tip: Pattern: If + present simple, ... will + base verb. It shows a real possibility, not something imaginary.
📌 Feelings Adjectives — "-ed" vs "-ing"
We use "-ed" adjectives to describe how a person feels, and "-ing" adjectives to describe the thing that causes the feeling.
Astyages was frightened by his dream.
The dream was frightening.
Harpagus felt shocked by the terrible command he received.
Tip: The person feels -ed (frightened); the thing itself is -ing (frightening). Do not mix them up!

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
😨
Fear can make people do terrible things. Astyages was so afraid of losing power that he ordered the death of his own grandson. What does this story teach us about the dangers of fear?
⚖️
Following orders is not always right. Harpagus felt he had no choice but to obey. When do you think it is right to refuse an order, even from someone powerful?
🔮
People often believe dreams are messages about the future. Many ancient rulers took their dreams very seriously. Why do you think people, then and now, look for meaning in their dreams?
👶
No one should be judged before they act. Astyages judged his grandson as dangerous before the baby had done anything at all. Why is it unfair to punish someone for something they have not yet done?
