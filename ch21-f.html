The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🏛️

Cyrus was not finished conquering yet. There was still one great enemy left standing in his way: Babylon. The Babylonians had ruled over Mesopotamia for a very long time, and theirs was an old, powerful kingdom. Cyrus wanted the good, fertile land between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers for himself — but he knew the Babylonian army was strong.

However, Cyrus had one enormous advantage. The Persian people loved Cyrus, because he was known to be a good and fair king. But the Babylonians, meanwhile, hated their own ruler. Their king had abandoned the city of Babylon completely, moving away to live in a distant desert. In his place, he had left his son, Belshazzar, in charge — and Belshazzar spent far too much time feasting and drinking, and far too little time caring for his own people.

So when Cyrus finally marched his army toward Babylon, he met very little resistance. The Babylonian people were sick and tired of their own careless king, and they had little reason to fight hard to protect him. Some of them were even willing to surrender — in fact, some Babylonians opened the city gates from the inside and let Cyrus’s army walk right in! Babylon fell to the Persians in 539 BC/BCE.

When Cyrus took control of Babylon, he also gained control of Canaan, the homeland of the Jewish people, who had been forced from their land years earlier by the Assyrians and Babylonians.

But Cyrus proved to be a generous king. Once he became ruler of Babylon, he allowed the Jewish people to return home to Canaan, and he let them worship their own god freely once again. This act of kindness made Cyrus even more popular. The Jewish people were so grateful to him that they gave him a remarkable title: "The Anointed of the Lord."

Now Cyrus stood as the greatest king in the entire known world. Yet there remained one land that still refused to obey him — Greece. And very soon, the Greeks and the Persians would meet each other in battle.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 "so...that" for result
We use "so + adjective + that" to explain the result of something being extreme.
Belshazzar spent so much time feasting that he ignored his people.
The Jews were so grateful that they gave Cyrus a special title.
The Babylonians were so unhappy that they opened the gates for Cyrus.
Tip: Pattern: so + adjective/adverb + that + result clause. It links a cause with a strong result.
📌 Passive Voice — "was/were + past participle"
We use the passive voice when the action is more important than who did it, or when the doer is unknown.
Babylon was conquered by Cyrus in 539 BC.
The gates were opened from the inside.
The Jewish people were allowed to return home.
Tip: Pattern: be + past participle. Compare: "Cyrus conquered Babylon" (active) vs "Babylon was conquered by Cyrus" (passive).
📌 "let" + person + base verb
We use "let" to show that someone gives permission for another person to do something.
Cyrus let the Jews go back to Canaan.
He let them worship their own god.
Cyrus let Median noblemen keep some power.
Tip: Pattern: let + person + base verb (no "to"). Compare with "allow," which needs "to": "He allowed them to go."

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
👑
Unpopular leaders weaken their own countries. Because Belshazzar ignored his people, Babylon fell almost without a fight. What does this teach us about the importance of good leadership during difficult times?
🕊️
Freedom of religion can win people’s loyalty. Cyrus allowed the Jewish people to worship freely, and they became loyal and grateful. Why do you think allowing people religious freedom can make a ruler more respected?
🌍
One empire’s ending can be another people’s new beginning. The fall of Babylon meant painful loss for some, but freedom and homecoming for the Jewish people. Why is it important to remember that historical events can mean very different things to different people?
⚔️
Every great empire eventually faces a new rival. Even after becoming the greatest king in the world, Cyrus still faced one nation that refused to obey him: Greece. Why do you think no empire in history has stayed unchallenged forever?

💡 Big Idea

Babylon fell easily because its own people had lost faith in their careless king, while Cyrus won lasting loyalty by freeing the Jewish people and letting them worship freely. A ruler’s greatest strength is often not their army, but the trust and gratitude of the people they govern.
