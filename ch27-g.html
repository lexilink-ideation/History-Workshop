The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
⚖️

Rome had seven kings. At first, the kings made the city stronger. They fought neighbors and learned from the Etruscans. They wore purple togas and carried the fasces. But after the seventh king, the people of Rome thought: "The king has too much power."

👑 They did not like living in a monarchy, a country where the king is in charge of everything. They wanted something different. The Romans wanted a place where the people could help make the laws and choose the leaders.

🏛️ Do you remember the Greek city that wanted the same thing? The city of Athens was a democracy. The people there voted about their laws and their leaders. In Athens, all men who were not slaves could vote. Would Rome copy Athens?

Neither consul could do exactly what he pleased, because the other consul was always there to stop him.

🤔 Not quite. Rome did not become a democracy like Athens. In Rome, only rich and powerful men, called patricians, were allowed to have a say in the government. Ordinary people did not have the same rights. This was an important difference.

🧑‍⚖️ Here is how the new government worked. The patricians appointed two of their own men as the leaders of the city. These leaders were called consuls. Why two? The Romans thought that having two leaders would stop one man from getting too much power. The two consuls were supposed to keep an eye on each other. Neither one could do exactly what he pleased!

🕰️ This idea was very clever. A king can make every decision alone. But two consuls had to agree, or at least not stop each other. The Romans used this idea for hundreds of years. Later, people in other countries also copied parts of the Roman government.

🌍 Now we have reached the end of the story of Rome's beginning. A tiny village on seven hills, a legend about twins and a wolf, seven kings who learned from the Etruscans, and a new government with two consuls. The best part of the story of Rome is still to come!

📝 Before you go on, try to explain the whole chapter to a friend in your own words: tell them about the empire pattern, the twins, the Etruscans, the fasces, and the two consuls. If you can do that, you have really understood the story of early Rome.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Comparing and contrasting: 'but', 'instead of', 'unlike'
Use these words to compare two systems.
Rome did not become a democracy; instead, it chose two consuls.
Unlike Athens, only patricians could take part.
In Athens, all free men could vote, but in Rome only patricians could.
💡 Tip: Contrast words help you explain how two things are different.
📌 Passive with 'were allowed to'
Use 'was/were allowed to' for what rules let people do.
Only patricians were allowed to have a say.
Ordinary people were not allowed to vote.
Two consuls were appointed by the patricians.
💡 Tip: allowed to + base verb.
📌 'Supposed to' for duties
Use 'supposed to' to talk about what people should do.
The consuls were supposed to keep an eye on each other.
The king was supposed to protect the people.
Judges are supposed to be fair.
💡 Tip: supposed to = expected to.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
👑
Too much power. The Romans feared one man with too much power. Can you think of a modern system that shares power among people?
🗳️
Who gets a vote? In Rome, only patricians had a say. Is it fair if only some people can take part in government?
👁️
Watching each other. The consuls had to watch one another. Are checks and balances a good idea in schools, families, or companies?
🔁
Learning from the past. The Romans copied ideas from Athens but changed them. Why do countries borrow and change ideas from others?

📖 Chapter 27 Summary

At first, Rome was just a tiny village in the hills of Italy. Empires like Assyria, Babylon, Persia, and Alexander's all fell, but Rome would grow bigger and last longer. The Romans told a legend of twin brothers, Romulus and Remus, who were thrown into the Tiber River in a basket, saved by a wolf and a shepherd, and who built a town on seven hills. Romulus killed his brother and became Rome's first king. Seven kings ruled Rome. They fought and traded with the Etruscans, learned painting and music from them, and borrowed the purple toga and the fasces, a symbol of royal power. But the people did not want one man to have too much power. Rome stopped being a monarchy and chose two consuls to lead it, appointed by the patricians.

💡 The Big Idea of Chapter 27

Rome began in legend, but it grew by learning from others and by finding a way to share power. A great state is built by ideas as well as by armies.
