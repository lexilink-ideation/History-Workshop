The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🧱

🏗️ The Romans were clever builders. They were the first ancient people to use concrete. They discovered that if they mixed volcanic ash, water, and lime, they got a soupy grey mixture. When this soupy mixture dried, it became as hard as stone!

🧱 The Romans used concrete like glue. They used it to hold big stones together in walls, bridges, and roads. They even built whole apartment buildings out of concrete. Some of these buildings were five stories high. That was very tall for the ancient world.

🐂 A Roman writer told a true and funny story about one of these tall buildings. On market day, an ox escaped from his owner. It ran through the busy streets and into an apartment building. Then it climbed the stairs, higher and higher and higher, until it reached the top floor. And then, the ox jumped out of a window!

⚠️ Not every Roman building was good, though. Some apartment buildings were built quickly and badly. Sometimes they collapsed, and the people inside were hurt or killed. Other buildings were slums. They had no water and no toilets. A whole family lived in one small room. People threw their waste out of the window into the street below. Watch your head!

The Romans built amazing things — but in a big city, not everyone lived well.

💧 As Roman cities grew bigger and bigger, the people needed more and more fresh water. So the Romans designed special channels for water called aqueducts. An aqueduct looked like a long, narrow stone bridge. Water ran along the top of it in pipes and channels. The aqueducts went gently downhill, so the water kept flowing. They could bring water into a city from springs thirty miles away!

🍲 The Romans needed water for drinking and cooking. But they also needed a lot of water for something they loved very much: taking baths. Most Romans took a bath every day.

🏊 But they did not have bathtubs at home. Instead, they went to the public baths. These were big buildings with pools, a little like a swimming centre today. People came to get clean and to meet their friends. First, they rubbed soap made from animal fat on their skin. Then they scraped the dirt off with special curved blades. After that, they could swim in hot water or cold water, all brought in by the aqueducts.

🏛️ The Romans built their roads, buildings, baths, and aqueducts more than two thousand years ago. But they built them so well that you can still visit their ruins today, in Italy and in many other countries where the Romans once lived.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 First conditional idea: 'if' + past for a discovery
Use 'if' to show that one thing causes another.
If they mixed ash, water, and lime, the mixture dried as hard as stone.
If a building was badly made, it could collapse.
If the city grew, people needed more water.
💡 Tip: Here 'if' talks about something that was true every time.
📌 Comparatives: bigger and bigger / more and more
Repeat a comparative to show something keeps changing.
The cities grew bigger and bigger.
People needed more and more water.
The ox climbed higher and higher.
The streets became busier and busier.
💡 Tip: Use this pattern to describe growth or change over time.
📌 Sequence words: first, then, after that
Use sequence words to describe steps in order.
First, they rubbed soap on their skin.
Then they scraped the dirt off.
After that, they swam in hot or cold water.
💡 Tip: Sequence words make instructions and stories easy to follow.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
💧
Water and cities. Rome needed aqueducts to bring water from far away. Where does the water in your city come from? What would happen without it?
🏚️
Rich and poor. Some Romans lived in marble houses, others in slums. Why do big cities often have both very rich and very poor people?
🧼
Keeping clean together. Romans met their friends at the baths. Why do you think public places like baths, parks, or libraries are important for a city?
🏛️
Built to last. Roman ruins still stand after 2,000 years. Should we build things today to last that long? Why or why not?
