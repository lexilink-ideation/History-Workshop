The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🧵

Athenian girls grew up very differently from Athenian boys. They were taught to become housewives. While some girls did learn to read and write, every girl learned domestic skills from her mother — how to manage a home, how to sew, how to grow a garden, and how to raise children and oversee household slaves. Athenian women were not allowed to vote, but they were expected to keep their households running smoothly while their husbands debated and voted at the forum.

One of the most famous men in Athens was a philosopher named Plato. Plato warned the Athenians that a true democracy needed educated citizens. If people remained ignorant, he said, those who knew more than they did could become tyrants and control everyone else.

Was Plato right? Imagine you had never been taught anything about ownership or stealing, and you were walking to the store with money to buy something you wanted.

Now imagine your neighbour, who is bigger and older than you, decides she wants your money for herself. "Do you not know that on this day of the week, smaller children must give their money to older children? It is the law!" she tells you. "If you refuse, you will be breaking the law." Because no one had ever taught you that there was no such rule, you might believe her, hand over your money, and watch her walk away with it.

This is exactly what Plato meant: an ignorant person can easily be persuaded to obey a tyrant, simply because they do not know any better. Athenians wanted to prevent tyrants from ever gaining that kind of power, so they worked hard to educate themselves and their children with real wisdom. The Spartans wanted to be strong and victorious above all else; the Athenians wanted to be wise and educated above all else. These were two very different Greek dreams.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Second Conditional — Imagining a Situation
We use the second conditional to imagine an unreal or hypothetical situation and its result.
If you had never been taught about stealing, you would not know it was wrong.
If people were ignorant, a tyrant could control them easily.
If citizens understood the law, they would not be so easily fooled.
Tip: Pattern: If + past simple, ... would/could + base verb. This describes an imagined situation, not a real one.
📌 "so that" for purpose (review)
We use "so that" to explain why someone does something.
Athenians educated their children so that tyrants could not fool them.
Plato wrote his ideas down so that others could learn from them.
People vote wisely so that good laws are passed.
Tip: Remember: "so that" is often followed by "can/could" or "will/would" to describe an intended result.
📌 Reported Speech with "warned that..."
We use "warned that" to report someone’s serious warning.
Plato warned that ignorant citizens could be controlled by tyrants.
He warned that democracy needed educated people.
Teachers warned students that laws must be understood, not just followed.
Tip: Pattern: warned (+ person) + that + clause. This reports a cautionary statement someone made.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🎓
Knowledge can be a form of protection. Plato believed that education protected people from being controlled by tyrants. Do you think education still protects people from unfair power today?
⚖️
Ancient societies often limited opportunities based on gender. Athenian girls, unlike boys, were mainly prepared only for domestic life. How have ideas about opportunities for men and women changed since ancient times?
🧠
A wise society questions the rules it is given. Plato’s story shows how easily people can be tricked if they never question what they are told. Why is questioning authority sometimes an important skill?
🏛️
Two neighbouring cities can hold two very different dreams. Sparta dreamed of strength and victory; Athens dreamed of wisdom and education. If you could choose only one of these two dreams for a whole society, which would you choose, and why?

💡 Big Idea

While Athenian girls were raised mainly for domestic life, the philosopher Plato taught that education and wisdom were the best protection any citizen — male or female — could have against being controlled by a tyrant. A society that values knowledge gives its people the power to think for themselves.
