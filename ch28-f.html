The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🔨

⚒️ Today's story is about a man called Servius. Servius is imaginary — he never really lived. But there were thousands of men just like him in ancient Rome. They became gladiators: men who fought each other as a game while crowds watched.

🌅 Servius lived in a small, quiet village near the sea. He was a metalworker. All day he made plows and hoes for the farmers, and fishhooks for the fishermen. He liked his work. At night he sat by the fire with his friends. They talked, sang songs, and told stories. Servius was happy.

🐎 One day, as Servius was heating metal for a new hoe, he heard a thundering noise. Was it going to rain? No! It was the sound of horses' hooves. Around the corner rode a group of men with swords, shields, and spears. They wore helmets and red cloaks.

⚔️ "We are Romans!" the biggest man shouted. "We claim this village for Rome! Now you must obey us!" Servius looked for a weapon, but all he could see was a hoe on the wall. He grabbed it and swung it at the man — but he missed. Two soldiers jumped from their horses and seized him from behind. "You are our prisoner!" the big man said. "You will come with us to Rome."

🛣️ For days and days, they rode down a wide road made of stone. At last Servius saw a city wall ahead. It was the highest wall he had ever seen. A man standing on another man's shoulders could not see over it!

🏙️ Inside the gate, Servius found himself in a narrow, crowded street. Little wooden booths lined both sides. People were selling fruit, meat, bread, cabbages, and cheap wine. Children chased a dog down the street. Babies cried, men shouted, and donkeys made loud noises. He had never seen so many people in one place!

🏛️ Soon the street grew wider. Servius saw green gardens, fountains, and houses of white marble. The people here wore fancy white togas with red and blue borders. "Where are you taking me?" he asked. "To the gladiator school," the soldier said. "You are big and strong. You will make a great fighter."

"You don't have any choice. You're our prisoner. Go to the gladiator school, or be executed."

😨 Servius felt his mouth go dry. "What if I don't want to be a gladiator?" The soldier answered, "Then you will be executed." Inside the school, men were training. One man threw a fishing net at his opponent and held a three-pointed spear. The other man, a "fish man", fought back with a short sword and a round shield. Servius's knees shook with terror. If this was only training, how could he ever survive a real fight?

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past continuous + past simple: an action interrupted
Use was/were + -ing for a longer action, and past simple for the short action that interrupted it.
Servius was heating metal when he heard a noise.
The children were chasing a dog when Servius arrived.
Men were training when Servius walked in.
💡 Tip: 'When' often joins the two parts.
📌 Superlative + present perfect: 'the highest ... he had ever seen'
Use the superlative with 'ever' to show something is more than anything before.
It was the highest wall he had ever seen.
It was the busiest street he had ever seen.
Rome was the biggest city he had ever visited.
💡 Tip: Because the story is in the past, we use 'had ever' not 'have ever'.
📌 'Or' for a hard choice
Use an imperative + 'or' + a result to show a warning.
Go to the gladiator school, or be executed.
Obey us, or you will be punished.
Run, or the soldiers will catch you.
💡 Tip: This structure sounds strong — it is often a threat or warning.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🏘️
Village and city. Servius was happy in his small village. Would you rather live in a quiet village or a big, busy city? Why?
⛓️
Freedom. Servius lost his freedom in one day. What does freedom mean to you? Why is it so important?
🎭
Imaginary stories. Servius is not real, but his story teaches us about real people. Can made-up stories help us understand history?
🛡️
Power over others. The soldiers said, 'Now you must obey us!' What gives one group the right to rule another? Does strength make it right?
