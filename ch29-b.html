2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
⚓

🏛️ When the Punic Wars began, Rome had a big problem. The Romans were excellent soldiers on land. They knew how to march, build camps, and fight in long, strong lines. But the war with Carthage was not only on land. Much of it was on the sea.

⛵ At first, Carthage had the advantage. Carthage had a navy. Its sailors had been sailing the Mediterranean for hundreds of years. They knew the winds, the waves, and the safe harbors. Their warships were fast and strong.

😟 And Rome? Rome didn't have a navy at all! The Romans were farmers and foot soldiers, not sailors. How could they fight an enemy that ruled the waves?

🌊 Then something lucky happened. One day, a Carthaginian warship wrecked on the coast of Italy. It crashed onto the shore and could not sail away. The Roman soldiers ran down to the beach and looked at it with great interest.

The Romans did not have a navy — so they copied their enemy's ship, piece by piece.

🔨 The Romans had a clever idea. They took the ship apart, board by board. They measured every piece of wood. They studied how the boards fitted together. Slowly, they figured out how the ship was built. Then they copied it.

🪵 Roman carpenters worked very hard. They cut down trees and built ship after ship. While the ships were being built, the new sailors practiced rowing on wooden benches on dry land! Each ship needed many rowers. They had to pull their oars at the same time, or the ship would go in circles.

🚢 At last the Romans had ships of their own. They learned how to sail them out on the real sea. Soon the Romans could match the Carthaginians in a sea battle. Carthage was surprised. Its enemy had learned to fight on the water!

💪 But Carthage was a tough enemy. Its people were rich, brave, and clever. The Romans had to work very hard to beat them. Many ships sank, and many sailors drowned in storms and battles.

🙏 The Romans believed that the gods decided who would win a war. So they made lots of sacrifices to their gods. They offered animals at the temples and prayed for victory. In the next lesson, you will meet a Roman general who took his prayers to sea in a very strange way — with chickens!

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Phrasal verbs: take apart, figure out
A phrasal verb is a verb + a small word. Together they have a new meaning.
They took the ship apart.
They figured out how it was built.
The ship broke up on the rocks.
The sailors set off across the sea.
💡 Tip: With an object, you can say 'took the ship apart' or 'took apart the ship'.
📌 Past continuous passive: 'while the ships were being built'
Use was/were being + past participle for an action happening to something at that time.
While the ships were being built, the sailors practiced.
The boards were being measured.
The oars were being made by carpenters.
The ship was being studied on the beach.
💡 Tip: This shows a long action in the past, done by someone else.
📌 'could' for ability in the past
Use 'could' + verb to say someone was able to do something in the past.
Soon the Romans could match the Carthaginians.
At first, Rome couldn't fight at sea.
The ship could not sail away.
Carthage's sailors could read the winds.
💡 Tip: 'Couldn't' = was not able to.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🔍
Learning from enemies. Rome learned shipbuilding from its enemy. What can we learn from people we compete against?
🛠️
Turning luck into success. A shipwreck was lucky for Rome, but the hard work made the difference. Is success more about luck or effort?
🚣
Teamwork. Rowers had to pull their oars together, or the ship went in circles. Where else in life must people work exactly together?
🙏
Asking for help. The Romans prayed to their gods for victory. Why do people look for help from something bigger than themselves in hard times?
