2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
☀️

The ancient North Americans did not write books, but they told wonderful stories. Here is one of them. It explains why rabbits are so timid.

🔥 It was the hottest day of summer. The sun beat down on the earth and turned it brown and dry. The grass withered. The animals were too hot and weary to run or play, so they lay in the shade, gasping for breath. Rabbit had searched for water all day. Every puddle was dry mud, and even the stream had trickled away into dust.

😠 Rabbit sat in the dry stream bed and shouted at the sun, "Stop shining! We need to cool off!" But the Sun paid no attention. Rabbit got hotter and thirstier. "Sun needs to learn a lesson," he grumbled. "I will take my bow and arrows and go east, where the Sun comes up. When he puts his head up tomorrow, I will shoot him!"

Rabbit, great Rabbit, enemy of the Sun. The Sun will learn my strength. Ho! Rabbit is coming!

🏹 In those days, the Sun did not rise slowly. He jumped into the sky with a great bound. So Rabbit ran east, singing his brave song. He waited all night under a tree. In the morning, the Sun sprang up, laughing. At once, Rabbit jumped up and shot an arrow right into the middle of the Sun!

💥 The arrow made a great hole in the Sun, and fire poured out over the world. The tree began to smoke. The grass burst into flames. Rabbit's fur began to scorch. In a panic, he threw down his bow and ran, crying, "Rabbit has shot the Sun! Fire is over the world!"

🌱 "Over here!" called a little voice. It was a tiny green bush. "Jump under me and you will be safe! I am so small that the fire will go right over me." Rabbit dived under the bush and buried his nose in the ground. The fire swept over him with a roar. When it was gone, the world was brown and burnt, and the bush was no longer green. It had turned yellow. We still call it the yellow bush.

🐇 Rabbit crept quietly away. To this day, Rabbit runs and hides when sunlight falls on him. And the Sun was never as bold as before. Instead of leaping up, he creeps up slowly, looking around for Rabbit and his bow.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past simple narrative (storytelling)
Stories about the past use past simple verbs in order.
Rabbit sat down, shouted at the Sun, and grabbed his bow.
The fire swept over him with a roar.
Rabbit crept away quietly.
💡 Tip: Irregular verbs: sit-sat, shoot-shot, run-ran, creep-crept, throw-threw.
📌 'At once' and time words
Words like 'at once', 'then', 'when' show the order of events.
At once, Rabbit jumped to his feet.
When the fire was gone, he looked around.
Soon the whole world was burning.
💡 Tip: Time words help your listener follow the story.
📌 Comparing with 'as ... as before'
Use 'not as ... as' to say something changed.
The Sun was not as bold as before.
The world was not as green as it had been.
Rabbit was not as brave as he thought.
💡 Tip: not as + adjective + as = less than.

📖 Chapter 26 Summary

The ancient Americas were home to many peoples who left no written records. In South America, the Nazca scraped enormous drawings of animals into the desert in Peru, and nobody knows exactly how or why. In Central America, the Olmecs built a city on a hill, a clay pyramid, and giant stone heads that still amaze us. In the cold north, hunters trapped animals and chased whales; on the plains, tribes followed the buffalo like nomads. Without books, they passed their stories down by voice, including the tale of Rabbit Shoots the Sun. Their artifacts and stories are the voices of people who never wrote their own history.

💡 The Big Idea of Chapter 26

The people of the Americas had no written records, but they left drawings, giant sculptures, and stories. History is found wherever people leave a trace, and every trace is a clue.
