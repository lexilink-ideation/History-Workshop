The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🏹

For hundreds of years, the story of the ancient world had been a story of empires rising and falling. First the Assyrians grew strong. Their king conquered city after city and forced everyone to obey him. Then the Babylonians grew stronger still, and for a while the Assyrians had to obey them instead. But the Assyrians did not enjoy being ruled by anyone else. They rebelled, took back their kingdom, and began conquering all over again.

Finally, two nations joined together to end the endless fighting: Babylon and a nation called Media. Together, the Babylonians and the Medes destroyed the Assyrian empire for good. For a while, Babylon and Media were the two most powerful nations in the whole region.

But history was not finished making surprises. Far to the east, at the edge of the Median kingdom, lived a small tribe of shepherds called the Persians. Nobody thought much about them. They spent their days watching sheep in the hills, and they had to obey the king of the Medes, a man named Astyages.

Astyages was not a kind ruler. He loved sitting on his throne, and he was willing to do terrible things to stay in power.

Astyages had a dream so frightening that he could not sleep again that night. In the dream, his own baby grandson grew up and took the throne away from him.

Terrified by the dream, Astyages called for his chief advisor, a man named Harpagus, as soon as the sun rose. He trusted Harpagus completely — or so he thought. Astyages was about to give him an order so cruel that it would set in motion the rise of the greatest empire the ancient world had ever seen.

Nobody in Media that morning could have guessed what was coming. The little shepherd tribe on the edge of the kingdom — the Persians — would soon have a new leader. And that leader would grow up to conquer the very king who feared him.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past Simple — Irregular Verbs
We use the past simple to talk about finished events in history. Many important verbs are irregular and do not add -ed.
Media and Babylon destroyed the Assyrian empire.
Astyages had a dream that frightened him.
The Persians were a small tribe of shepherds.
Tip: Irregular verbs like have→had and be→were change their form completely. They must be learned by heart, not by adding -ed.
📌 because / so (cause and effect)
We use "because" to give a reason, and "so" to give a result.
The Assyrians rebelled because they did not want to obey Babylon.
Astyages was frightened, so he called his advisor.
The Persians had to obey Media because Media was more powerful.
Tip: "Because" answers the question "Why?" and "so" introduces the result. Try switching the order: "Because the Assyrians did not want to obey Babylon, they rebelled."
📌 Superlatives — the most / the -est
We use superlatives to say something is number one in a whole group.
Babylon and Media became the most powerful nations in the region.
Astyages wanted to stay the most important man in his kingdom.
The little Persian tribe seemed like the least dangerous group of all.
Tip: For longer adjectives, use "the most / the least + adjective." For short adjectives, add "-est" (for example, strong → the strongest).

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
👑
Power can make people afraid. Astyages already had a throne, an army, and a kingdom — yet a single dream terrified him. Why do you think powerful people are sometimes the most afraid of losing what they have?
🔄
History repeats in cycles. In this chapter, power passed from the Assyrians to the Babylonians and back again, again and again. Why do you think history often repeats similar patterns?
🐑
Small beginnings can lead to big changes. The Persians started as a tiny, unimportant tribe of shepherds. What other examples do you know of small beginnings leading to big changes?
🤔
A warning can change the future. Astyages’s dream was only a warning, not a fact. Do you think trying to stop a bad future from happening can sometimes cause that very future to happen?
