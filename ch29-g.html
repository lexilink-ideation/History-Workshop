The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🏳️

🛡️ After Scipio defeated Hannibal's tired army in Africa, Carthage had no strong soldiers left. The great city could not fight any longer. The leaders of Carthage were forced to surrender to Rome. To surrender means to stop fighting and say, "You have won."

📜 Rome made Carthage pay a heavy price. Carthage had to give the Romans large amounts of money. It had to give up most of its warships. And it promised never to start a war again without Rome's permission. The proud trading city, which the Phoenicians had built long ago, had lost its pride.

🌅 Far away in Asia Minor, Hannibal was still hiding. One day, the news reached him: Carthage had surrendered to Rome. Hannibal was heartbroken. He had crossed the Alps with elephants. He had won battle after battle in Italy. Now his city belonged to his enemies.

Hannibal could not bear to think that his great city had been beaten by Rome.

💔 Hannibal could not bear this sad news. He did not want to live as a prisoner of the Romans. So, at the end of his life, the great general took poison. This was a sad end for one of the most famous generals in history. Even the Romans, who feared him, remembered his name for hundreds of years.

⏳ The Punic Wars were very, very long. The First Punic War was fought from 264 to 241 BC. The Second Punic War, the war of Hannibal and his elephants, was fought from 218 to 202 BC. Later there was a third war. Finally, in 146 BC, the Romans destroyed the city of Carthage. The fighting had lasted for more than a century — over one hundred years!

🌊 Now Rome had no more rivals in the western Mediterranean. Roman ships could sail anywhere. Roman traders could buy and sell in every port. Rome had become the master of the Mediterranean Sea.

🔁 Think back over this whole chapter. Rome and Carthage both wanted more land and more trade. At first, Carthage was stronger at sea. But the Romans copied a wrecked ship and built a navy. A Roman general threw sacred chickens into the sea and lost a battle. Then Hannibal surprised Rome with elephants in the snow of the Alps. Finally, Scipio's clever plan brought Hannibal home, and Rome won.

🏛️ Rome had started as a tiny village in the hills. Now it was the most powerful city in the western world. And Rome was not finished growing yet!

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Passive with 'was forced to'
Use 'was/were forced to' + verb when someone had to do something they did not want to do.
Carthage was forced to surrender.
The city was forced to give up its ships.
Hannibal was forced to leave Italy.
💡 Tip: 'Forced to' is stronger than 'had to' — there was no choice at all.
📌 Dates and periods: from ... to ..., for ...
Use 'from ... to ...' for start and end dates, and 'for' for the length of time.
The First Punic War was fought from 264 to 241 BC.
The Second Punic War lasted from 218 to 202 BC.
The fighting lasted for more than a century.
💡 Tip: With BC dates, the numbers get smaller as time goes forward.
📌 Past perfect for looking back
Use 'had + past participle' when looking back at earlier events in a story.
Hannibal had crossed the Alps with elephants.
He had won battle after battle in Italy.
Rome had started as a tiny village.
💡 Tip: Use it to remind your listener of what happened earlier.

📖 Chapter 29 Summary

Rome and Carthage both wanted to control trade around the Mediterranean Sea, so they fought the long Punic Wars from 264 to 146 BC. At first Carthage was stronger because it had a navy, but the Romans copied a wrecked Carthaginian ship and learned to fight at sea. The Roman general Claudius Pulcher threw his seasick sacred chickens overboard and then lost a battle. When the war at sea became a stalemate, the Carthaginian general Hannibal marched his army and forty elephants over the Alps and invaded Italy in 218 BC. The Romans were terrified until Scipio sailed to Africa and attacked Carthage itself. Hannibal had to come home, and his worn-out army was defeated. Carthage surrendered, Hannibal took poison in Asia Minor, and Rome became the master of the Mediterranean.

💡 The Big Idea of Chapter 29

Rome won the Punic Wars by learning from its enemy, by never giving up, and by thinking cleverly. Hard work and new ideas helped a city of farmers become master of the sea.
