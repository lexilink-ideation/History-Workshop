The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🏟️

The ancient Olympics were far more than a set of races. People came from all over Greece not only to compete but also to watch. These spectators camped out at the Games and spent their evenings feasting and listening to music. Poets recited long poems and stories out loud — these were like the movies of the ancient world. Some poets probably told the tale of the Odyssey, or of the attack on Troy.

Winning athletes were treated like heroes. They were honoured with a great banquet, and when they returned home, their cities rewarded them with money and free food for the rest of their lives. To be an Olympic champion was one of the greatest honours a Greek could earn.

The Olympics were held every four years for almost a thousand years — and amazingly, they never truly disappeared. Today we still celebrate the Games, keeping alive a tradition thousands of years old.

The modern Olympics are held every four years, just like in ancient times. Some events are the same: wrestling, running, and boxing. But hundreds of new sports have been added too, such as gymnastics, ice skating, soccer, basketball, and swimming. The athletes still train to be strong and graceful, just as the ancient Greeks did.

There are big differences too. Today, women compete as well as men. And athletes come from all over the planet, not only from Greece — the Games are now a truly worldwide event. Yet the name has never changed. They are still called the Olympics, after Mount Olympus.

From wild barbarians who could not write, to the alphabet, to Homer's poems, to the Olympic Games — the Greeks built a civilisation whose ideas still shape our world. The Olympics are a living legacy of ancient Greece, still celebrating strength, grace, and courage, just as they did thousands of years ago.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 used to (past habits)
We use 'used to' for something that was true in the past but is not true now.
Only men used to compete in the Games.
Athletes used to receive an olive wreath.
The Games used to be only for Greeks.
Tip: Pattern: used to + base verb. It shows a change from past to present.
📌 Comparing past and present
We use signal words to compare then and now.
In the pastToday
Only men competed.Both men and women compete.
Only Greeks took part.Athletes come from all over the world.
There were a few events.There are hundreds of events.
Tip: Useful signal words: in the past, back then, today, now, these days.
📌 still / no longer
We use 'still' for things that continue, and 'no longer' for things that have stopped.
The Games are still called the Olympics.
Women are no longer banned from the Games.
Athletes still train to be strong and graceful.
Tip: still = it continues; no longer = it has stopped.

Chapter Review
Look back at the whole chapter!
📜 Chapter 20 Summary

In Chapter 20, wild tribes settled in Greece and slowly became civilised Greeks. They learned to read and write, borrowing letters from the Phoenicians to build their own alphabet. The blind poet Homer wrote down the great stories of the Iliad and the Odyssey — including Odysseus's clever escape from the Cyclops. Finally, the Greeks created the Olympic Games, a festival of peace and strength held every four years. From the alphabet to the Olympics, ancient Greece left gifts that still shape our world today.

💡 Big Idea

Ancient Greece began with people who could not even write their own names, and ended by giving the world its alphabet, its greatest stories, and the Olympic Games. Civilisations are built slowly, but the best of what they create — ideas, stories, and traditions — can last for thousands of years.
