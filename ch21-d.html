The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🐑

High on the mountainside, the shepherd boy named Cyrus grew tall and strong. He was faster and cleverer than any other boy his age, and he seemed to stand head and shoulders above everyone around him. One day, his adopted father took him down from the mountains into the town, to help sell sheep in the busy marketplace.

While Cyrus was there selling sheep, King Astyages himself happened to pass through the marketplace, together with his advisor Harpagus. The moment Astyages saw the tall young boy, something about him seemed strangely familiar — and slowly, the king began to discover the truth.

That evening, Astyages called for Harpagus. "You disobeyed me!" the king shouted. Harpagus finally admitted that he had given the baby to a shepherd all those years ago, instead of ending his life.

Astyages was furious. He wanted to punish Harpagus and his entire family for their disobedience. But Harpagus managed to flee to the mountains before the king’s soldiers could catch him — and there, he found Cyrus, now a grown man.

"If you want to take the king’s power away and become ruler of the Persians yourself," Harpagus told Cyrus, "I will help you do it." Cyrus listened carefully. Together, the two of them worked to convince the Persian people to follow Cyrus instead of his grandfather.

Cyrus took power away from Astyages, exactly as the old king had feared in his dream so many years before. Then Cyrus led the Persians in a war against the powerful empire of Media. After three long years of fierce fighting, Cyrus finally defeated the king of Media as well.

Cyrus, the boy who had been raised by a humble shepherd on a lonely mountain, now stood as the ruler of the combined armies and lands of the Medes and the Persians. His grandfather’s frightening dream had finally come true.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past Perfect — "had disobeyed"
We use the past perfect to talk about something that happened before another past event.
Harpagus admitted that he had disobeyed the king.
Cyrus discovered that he had been a prince all along.
By the time Astyages found out, Harpagus had already fled.
Tip: Pattern: had + past participle. It shows which past action happened first.
📌 Reflexive Structure — "convince someone to..."
We use "convince + person + to + verb" to show that someone was persuaded to do something.
Harpagus and Cyrus convinced the Persians to follow Cyrus.
Cyrus convinced his people to fight for freedom.
No one could convince Astyages to change his mind.
Tip: Pattern: convince + person + to + base verb. It is similar to "persuade someone to do something."
📌 Time Expressions — "after three years"
We use time expressions with "after" to show how much time passed before something happened.
After three years of fighting, Cyrus defeated Media.
After discovering the truth, Astyages was furious.
Years after the dream, it finally came true.
Tip: Time expressions like "after + time period" help readers understand how much time has passed in a story.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🔄
You cannot always escape your fate by fighting it. Astyages tried everything to stop his dream from coming true, but it happened anyway. What does this story suggest about trying to control the future?
🤝
Betrayal can also lead to justice. Harpagus disobeyed his king years earlier, and later helped overthrow him completely. Was Harpagus right to help Cyrus take revenge?
👦
People often underestimate others because of where they come from. No one suspected that a simple shepherd boy could one day become a great ruler. Why do you think people sometimes judge others by their background rather than their ability?
⚔️
Power can pass peacefully or violently between generations. In this story, power passed from grandfather to grandson through war. Can you think of other ways power can change hands, both peacefully and violently?

💡 Big Idea

The shepherd boy Cyrus grew up, was discovered by his own grandfather, and eventually rose up to defeat him and unite the Medes and Persians under his rule. Trying too hard to control the future can sometimes be exactly what causes it to happen.
