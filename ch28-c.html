How six pomegranate seeds gave the world winter and spring

The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
👑

⚡ Ceres stood in the palace of Jupiter, waiting for his answer. The earth below was already brown and dying. Jupiter thought for a long time. At last he spoke.

🍽️ 'Very well,' he said. 'I will tell Pluto to let your daughter go — on one condition. Proserpine can leave the Underworld only if she has not eaten or drunk anything in Pluto's palace. But if she ate or drank there, she must stay.' This was an old rule. Anyone who ate the food of the dead belonged to the land of the dead.

🌋 Suddenly, the earth cracked open. Out of the darkness came Proserpine. Beside her stood a tall, dark man in a black cloak. It was Pluto, the lord of the Underworld.

🤗 'My daughter!' Ceres cried. She ran forward to hug her.

✋ 'Wait,' said Jupiter. 'Proserpine, tell me the truth. Have you eaten or drunk anything in the Underworld?'

🍎 Proserpine looked down. 'Hardly anything,' she said in a small voice. 'I only ate six seeds from a pomegranate, just a few minutes ago.'

😟 Jupiter shook his head. 'Then you must stay with Pluto,' he said.

💪 But Ceres refused to give in. She stood tall in front of the king of the gods. 'If I do not get my daughter back,' she warned, 'spring will never come again. Nothing will grow. The people will starve.'

She ate six seeds, so she stays six months below — and the world above has winter.

⚖️ Jupiter considered the case carefully. He wanted to be fair to Pluto, to Ceres, and to the people on earth. Finally, he made his decision. 'She only ate six seeds,' he said. 'So for six months — half of the year — she must stay in the Underworld with Pluto. But for the other six months, she may live with her mother in the world above.'

❄️ And so, the Romans said, Proserpine spends six months of every year in the Underworld. While she is in Pluto's palace, her mother mourns and weeps. The leaves fall from the trees, the grass turns brown, and the flowers die. Cold winds blow. This is autumn and winter.

🌼 But each spring, Proserpine comes back to her mother, and Ceres rejoices. The trees grow new leaves. The grass turns green again. Flowers bud and bloom, and farmers plant their crops. This is spring and summer.

🔭 Today we know that the seasons change because the earth is tilted as it travels around the sun. But for the Romans, this beautiful story explained why the world grows cold and then warm again, year after year.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Present perfect questions: Have you ever...?
Use have/has + past participle to ask about something that happened at any time up to now.
Have you eaten anything in the Underworld?
Has she drunk anything?
Have you seen my daughter?
Have you ever tasted a pomegranate?
💡 Tip: Answer with 'Yes, I have' or 'No, I haven't'.
📌 Only + quantity
Put 'only' before a number or amount to show it is small.
I only ate six seeds.
She ate hardly anything.
She stays only half of the year.
Jupiter gave her only one condition.
💡 Tip: 'Hardly anything' means 'almost nothing'.
📌 Present simple for things that happen every year
Use the present simple to explain habits and repeated events.
Proserpine spends six months in the Underworld.
Ceres mourns and the leaves fall.
Every spring, Proserpine comes back.
The grass turns green again.
💡 Tip: Remember the -s with he, she, and it: she spends, it turns.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🤝
The art of compromise. Jupiter gave each side half of what they wanted. When is a compromise the best answer, and when is it not?
🍎
Small actions, big results. Six small seeds changed Proserpine's whole life. Can you think of a small choice that had a big result?
🔭
Myth and science. The Romans used a story to explain the seasons. Today we use science. Why do you think people still enjoy the old story?
😢
Seasons of feeling. Ceres is sad in winter and happy in spring. Do the seasons change how people feel? How?
