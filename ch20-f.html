2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🏅

The Greeks loved to celebrate courage and strength. They told stories about brave heroes like Odysseus — and they also held a huge festival called the Olympic Games. The bravest and strongest Greeks came from all over to compete for prizes.

How did the Olympics begin? Two Greek cities had fought each other for years and years. At last they made peace. To celebrate, they held a great festival to honour Zeus, the chief god of the Greeks. The festival was named after Mount Olympus, the highest mountain in Greece, where the Greeks believed the gods lived.

At the festival, the Greeks feasted and made a sacrifice to Zeus. Then they ran races. The winners were given a wreath of olive leaves — and the olive leaves stood for peace.

The Greeks decided to hold the Olympics every four years. As time went on, more and more Greeks came, and more events were added. Instead of only foot races, they held horse races, boxing, and wrestling. They even invented the pentathlon, a contest of five events. To win it, an athlete had to throw the discus and the javelin farther than anyone, and also win a long jump, a wrestling match, and a foot race.

But there was one strict rule: only men could compete. Girls could watch, but they were not allowed to race. Married women could not even come near the Games. The Greeks believed that only men could be truly brave and strong, and that the best way to honour the gods was for men to train their bodies to be graceful and powerful.

Winners were treated like heroes. They were given great feasts, and when they went home, their cities rewarded them with money and free food for life. The Olympics were held every four years for almost a thousand years — a festival of strength, peace, and honour that would never be forgotten.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Passive Voice (past)
We use the passive when the action matters more than who did it: be + past participle.
The festival was named after Mount Olympus.
Winners were given an olive wreath.
The Games were held every four years.
Tip: Pattern: was/were + past participle. Use it when the doer is unknown or unimportant.
📌 Frequency: 'every four years'
We use 'every + time period' to show how often something happens.
The Olympics were held every four years.
The Greeks feasted every evening at the Games.
New events were added every so often.
Tip: Pattern: every + number + plural time word (every four years, every two days).
📌 only / but for limits
We use 'only' and 'but' to show a limit or an exception.
Only men could compete in the Games.
Girls could watch, but they could not race.
There was only one strict rule about who could take part.
Tip: only narrows things down; but introduces an exception.

💡 Big Idea

The Olympic Games began when two Greek cities made peace and held a festival to honour Zeus. Athletes competed in races, wrestling, boxing, and the pentathlon, and winners received an olive wreath — a symbol of peace. Only men could take part. The Olympics turned strength and courage into a celebration of peace, held every four years for almost a thousand years.
