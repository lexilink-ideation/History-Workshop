2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
❄️

Now let us travel up from Central America into North America. Today this big continent has two countries, Canada and the United States. But in ancient times, many different tribes roamed all over it. Each tribe lived in a way that fit the land around it.

🥶 Way up in the far north, the weather is very cold. The ground is frozen for most of the year. It was too cold to grow crops, so the ancient people there lived by hunting and fishing. They had to be clever to survive. They trapped the animals all around them: seals, polar bears, birds, and caribou. Caribou look a little like elk or antelopes.

🌿 The people also gathered mosses and lichens. These are small plants that grow in the cold north, and people could eat them. Nothing was wasted. Animal skins became warm clothes, and bones became tools.

A whale could provide enough meat for an entire village, and its fat made oil for lamps.

🐋 The bravest hunters even went out onto the icy sea in boats made from animal skins. They fished and chased whales. A whale was a big prize. One whale could give enough meat for a whole village. The thick fat, called blubber, was melted into oil. People burned the oil in lamps to give light and heat during the long, dark winter.

🛶 Think about how dangerous this was. The water was cold enough to kill a person in minutes. The boats were light and easy to turn over. A hunter needed courage, skill, and good friends in the boat. When the hunt was successful, the whole village shared the food and celebrated together.

🏕️ These northern people had no farms, no cities, and no great stone buildings. Yet they were not poor in knowledge. They knew the weather, the stars, the ice, and the animals. This knowledge was their most important tool, and parents taught it to their children.

🔥 The story of the far north shows us something important. People can live almost anywhere on Earth if they learn how to use what the land gives them. The cold north did not stop the ancient North Americans. It made them strong and clever.

🌊 Today, some Arctic peoples still hunt and fish in ways they learned from their grandparents. They remind us that the ancient North Americans were not simple. They were experts in snow, ice, wind, and water, and they solved hard problems every day.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Lists with commas and 'and'
Use commas for three or more things; put 'and' before the last one.
They trapped seals, polar bears, birds, and caribou.
They fished, hunted, and gathered plants.
The land gave them meat, skins, and oil.
💡 Tip: Commas separate items; 'and' comes before the last item.
📌 Present simple for facts
Use the present simple to describe things that are always true.
The weather in the far north is very cold.
Caribou look like elk.
Mosses and lichens grow in cold places.
💡 Tip: Add -s with he/she/it: a whale gives, the sun shines.
📌 'Enough' and 'too'
'Too' means more than needed. 'Enough' means as much as needed.
It was too cold to grow crops.
A whale gave enough meat for a village.
The water was too cold to swim in.
💡 Tip: too + adjective; enough + noun / adjective + enough.
