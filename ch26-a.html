2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🌎

We have already read about the people of Europe, Africa, and Asia. Now it is time to travel to the other side of the world! Long ago, ancient people also lived there, in the Americas.

🧭 Put your finger on the Fertile Crescent. Now move it to the left. You will cross the Mediterranean Sea and then the huge Atlantic Ocean. At last you will find two big continents. The top one is North America. The bottom one is South America. A narrow strip of land joins them in the middle. We call it Central America.

📜 There is one big problem with the people of the Americas. Like the people of ancient Africa, they did not leave written records. So we cannot read their own stories. We know much less about them than we know about the Egyptians, the Babylonians, or the Greeks. But they did leave artifacts: old buildings, ruined villages, and mysterious earth mounds.

The people of the Americas did not write books, but they left buildings, villages, and mounds for us to study.

⛰️ South America has tall mountains along one edge. In the middle, there is flat and fertile land. Different tribes lived in the mountains and in the hot, wet jungles of the flat lands. Their lives were like the lives of people in ancient Mesopotamia. They grew crops, kept animals, hunted, and caught fish.

🥔 They also ate cassava, just like the people of ancient Africa! They learned how to dry the cassava roots and grind them into a powder. Then they made the powder into a soft pudding. You may have eaten it yourself. It is called tapioca pudding!

🌿 One of these South American tribes was called the Nazca. They lived along the rivers in a place that is now called Peru. The Nazca left behind one of the strangest mysteries of the ancient world. In the next lesson, you will find out what it is.

❓ Think about this: if a people leave no books, how can we learn about them? Scientists look carefully at the things the people left in the ground. Every broken pot and every pile of earth is a small clue.

🔍 Archaeologists are scientists who study the past by digging in the ground. They carefully brush away the dirt and measure what they find. Slowly, they can say how old a village is and what its people ate. This work takes many years, but it helps us hear voices that have been quiet for thousands of years.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past simple for finished actions
We use the past simple for things that happened and finished long ago.
The Nazca lived along the rivers.
Ancient people grew crops and caught fish.
They did not leave written records.
💡 Tip: Add -ed to regular verbs. Learn irregular verbs like grow-grew and leave-left.
📌 Comparing with 'like' and 'just like'
Use 'like' to show two things are the same.
Like the people of Mesopotamia, they grew crops.
They ate cassava, just like the people of ancient Africa.
Like the Africans, they left no written records.
💡 Tip: Put 'like' at the start of a sentence, then a comma.
📌 Position words: along, across, between
Use these words to explain where places are.
Mountains run along one edge of South America.
You sail across the Atlantic Ocean.
Central America is between North and South America.
💡 Tip: Look at a map while you practise.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
📜
Writing keeps memories. Many ancient peoples wrote nothing down. What do we lose when a people has no written records?
🏺
Objects can talk. A broken pot can teach scientists about food and daily life. What object from your home would tell a stranger about you?
🌍
Civilizations grew all over the world. People in Asia, Africa, and the Americas built villages at about the same time. Why do you think this happened?
🥣
Food travels and connects people. Tapioca comes from cassava, a food of both Africa and South America. Which food do people in different countries share today?
