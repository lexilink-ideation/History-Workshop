2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
📜

When the Greeks first learned to read and write, one of them decided to write down the old stories that people had told out loud around the fire for years and years. His name was Homer, and he was the first great Greek poet.

Tradition tells us that Homer was blind. Because he could not see, he listened very carefully to the stories he heard — and then he wrote them down using the new Greek alphabet. In this way, some very old tales were saved forever.

Homer wrote about a famous war: the Greek attack on the city of Troy. This was called the Trojan War, and Homer told the story in a long poem, the Iliad.

After he finished the Iliad, Homer wrote a second great poem, the Odyssey. It was about Odysseus, a clever Greek warrior who had fought in the Trojan War. When the war ended, Odysseus and his men set sail for home — but their voyage did not go as planned!

At first the men were happy. They praised all the gods of Greece for keeping them alive through the war. They thanked the gods for their victory over Troy. But there was a problem. They forgot to thank one very important god — Poseidon, the god of the sea.

Poseidon was furious at being left out. He sent a strong wind to blow the ships off course, so that Odysseus would have a hard time getting home. Odysseus and his tired, hungry men got lost, far out on the wide sea. Their long adventure was only just beginning — and it would take ten long years before Odysseus saw his home again.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past Simple in storytelling
Stories about the past use the past simple to tell events in order.
BasePastFrom the story
writewroteHomer wrote the Iliad.
sendsentPoseidon sent a strong wind.
forgetforgotThey forgot to thank Poseidon.
beginbeganTheir adventure began.
Tip: Many common storytelling verbs are irregular — practise them until they feel natural.
📌 'so' to show a result
We use 'so' to connect a cause with its result.
Poseidon was angry, so he sent a storm.
Homer was blind, so he listened carefully.
The wind blew them off course, so they got lost.
Tip: Pattern: cause + so + result. It answers the question 'What happened next?'
📌 'forget / decide + to' (infinitive)
Some verbs are followed by 'to' + the base verb.
They forgot to thank Poseidon.
One Greek decided to write down the stories.
Odysseus wanted to sail home.
Tip: After forget, decide, want, hope, need, use to + verb.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🎭
Stories carry a people's memory. For years, Greek tales lived only in people's voices. Why do you think stories are so important to a culture's sense of who they are?
👂
A weakness can become a strength. Homer was blind, yet he became a master storyteller. Can you think of a time when a difficulty helped someone develop a special skill?
🙏
The Greeks believed the gods could be offended. Odysseus's trouble began when the men forgot to thank a god. Why do you think ancient people believed the gods watched their every action?
🌊
One small mistake can lead to big trouble. Forgetting to thank Poseidon cost Odysseus ten years. Do you think small choices can really change the whole path of a life?

💡 Big Idea

Homer, the first great Greek poet, wrote down the old stories in two long poems: the Iliad, about the Trojan War, and the Odyssey, about the warrior Odysseus. When Odysseus's men forgot to thank Poseidon, the angry sea god sent storms that kept them lost for ten years. Great stories can survive for thousands of years — if someone takes the time to write them down.
