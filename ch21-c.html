The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🐐

Harpagus walked slowly toward the mountains, carrying the baby prince in his arms. His king had given him a secret command: get rid of the child, and never tell anyone. But the closer Harpagus got to the wild mountainside, the harder it became for him to go through with the plan.

"I must obey my king," he told himself. "I must do this. But I cannot make myself do it!" No matter how hard he tried, Harpagus simply could not bring himself to harm an innocent baby.

Just then, he noticed a shepherd nearby, grazing his sheep on the hillside. Harpagus called the man over and made a decision. "Here," he said, holding out the baby. "The king wants this child gone. You must do it. If you finish the job, I will give you great wealth as a reward. But if you refuse, the king’s soldiers will come and punish you instead."

The shepherd looked down at the baby in his arms — and had an idea of his own. "All right," he said to Harpagus. "I will get rid of the baby." He made a quiet promise to himself instead.

The shepherd ran home to his wife, who had never been able to have children of her own. "Wife!" he called out. "The gods have sent us a son! We can raise him as our own child!"

His wife took the baby into her arms with joyful tears streaming down her face. She had wanted a child for so long, and now one had come to her in the strangest way. The shepherd knew he had to hide the truth from Harpagus, so he killed a young goat, wiped its blood on his hands as false evidence, and ran back to report that the terrible task was finished.

The shepherd and his merciful wife named the baby boy Cyrus, and they began to raise him high on the quiet mountainside, never once suspecting how much the world would one day hear his name.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past Continuous — for background actions
We use the past continuous to describe an action already in progress when something else happened.
A shepherd was grazing his sheep when Harpagus arrived.
The baby was crying as Harpagus carried him.
While the wife was waiting, her husband ran home with news.
Tip: Pattern: was/were + verb-ing. It sets the scene for another action or event in the past.
📌 could not / couldn’t + base verb
We use "could not" to say someone was unable to do something, even if they tried.
Harpagus could not bring himself to harm the baby.
He could not disobey the king completely, either.
The couple could not have children of their own.
Tip: "Could not" describes a lack of ability in the past, whether physical or emotional.
📌 Direct Speech — Quotation Marks
We use quotation marks to show someone’s exact words.
"Wife!" he said. "The gods have sent us a son!"
"All right," the shepherd said. "I will get rid of the baby."
"Here," Harpagus said, "take the child."
Tip: Put a comma or punctuation before the closing quotation mark, and start the quoted sentence with a capital letter.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
❤️
Small acts of mercy can change history. If the shepherd had obeyed Harpagus completely, Cyrus would never have grown up to build an empire. How can one small, kind decision change the future?
🎭
People sometimes have to deceive others to do what they believe is right. The shepherd lied to Harpagus to protect the baby. Is it ever right to lie in order to protect someone?
👨‍👩‍👦
Love does not always come from blood family. The shepherd and his wife loved Cyrus like their own son, even though he was not born to them. What makes someone truly a parent?
🌄
Ordinary people can play a part in extraordinary events. A simple shepherd and his wife, without knowing it, protected the future ruler of a great empire. Why is it important to remember the ordinary people hidden inside big historical stories?
