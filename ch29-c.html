2
The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🐔

⚔️ The war between Rome and Carthage went on and on. The Romans now had ships, but every battle was hard. Many Romans believed that the gods decided who would win. So before a battle, they always tried to find out what the gods were thinking.

🧑‍✈️ One Roman general was named Claudius Pulcher. He was the leader of a Roman fleet. Before he sailed out to fight Carthage, he did something that seems very strange to us. He took a cage of sacred chickens with him on his ship!

🐓 Why chickens? The Romans believed that the gods could send messages through animals. Claudius Pulcher hoped that the sacred chickens would bring him good fortune in battle. He also thought that he could foretell the future by watching the way the chickens ate.

🌾 Here is how it worked. Before a battle, a priest threw grain to the chickens. If the chickens ate hungrily, the gods were happy, and the Romans would win. But if the chickens would not eat, the gods were angry. That meant the battle would go badly.

🌊 Unfortunately, a ship on the sea goes up and down, up and down, all day long. The chickens were not used to the waves. They got seasick! They sat in their cage with their feathers puffed up, and they would not eat at all.

The chickens were only seasick — but the soldiers believed the gods were against them.

😨 This was a very bad sign. The Roman soldiers on the ship got more and more nervous. "The gods are against us!" they whispered to each other. "We can tell, because the chickens aren't eating! We are doomed to fail!"

😤 Claudius Pulcher got more and more irritated. He threw more grain into the cage. He waited. He shouted. Nothing he did would make those chickens eat. At last he lost his temper. "If they won't eat," he cried, "then let them drink! Throw the chickens overboard!"

💦 Splash! The sacred chickens went into the sea.

💥 Sure enough, Claudius Pulcher was badly beaten in the next battle. Many of his ships were captured or sunk. And all of his soldiers believed they had lost because they had thrown the sacred chickens into the sea.

🤔 Was it really the chickens? Probably not! The soldiers were frightened before the battle even started, and frightened soldiers do not fight well. A belief like this, in luck or magic without real facts, is called a superstition. Sometimes what we believe can change what we do.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 'more and more' + adjective
Use 'more and more' to show a feeling growing stronger and stronger.
The soldiers got more and more nervous.
Claudius got more and more irritated.
The sea became rougher and rougher.
The chickens looked sicker and sicker.
💡 Tip: Short adjectives use -er and -er (rougher and rougher).
📌 'would not' for refusing
Use 'would not' (wouldn't) to say someone or something refused to do something.
The chickens wouldn't eat at all.
Nothing he did would make them eat.
The soldiers would not stop whispering.
He wouldn't listen to the priest.
💡 Tip: 'Wouldn't' here means 'refused to', not the future.
📌 Past perfect: 'had thrown'
Use had + past participle for an action that happened before another past action.
They lost because they had thrown the chickens into the sea.
The soldiers were scared because the chickens had refused to eat.
He was angry because he had waited so long.
The ships had sailed far from Italy.
💡 Tip: Past perfect = 'the earlier past'.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🔮
Predicting the future. The Romans watched chickens to see the future. How do people try to predict the future today? Which ways are scientific?
🧠
Belief and confidence. The soldiers lost hope before the battle began. How can what we believe change how well we do?
🍀
Lucky and unlucky. Many cultures have lucky numbers or colors. What superstitions are common where you live? Where did they come from?
😤
Losing your temper. Claudius Pulcher got angry and threw the chickens away. Was this a good leader's choice? What should a leader do when the team is afraid?
